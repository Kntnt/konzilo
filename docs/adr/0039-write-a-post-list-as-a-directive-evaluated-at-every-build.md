# ADR 0039: Write a post list as a directive evaluated at every build

**Date:** 2026-10-29, amended 2026-11-11, 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13 · **Status:** Accepted. Settled by Thomas on 2026-10-29: a query component with a query builder; evaluated at build; open to fully dynamic lists later; shown by presentations the site registers; no archive by default. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-10: related posts by shared topics and category times freshness (ADR 0070). The rest is a technical choice; Thomas may veto. Among it, settled by the agent on 2026-11-12: what a presentation is given of the whole list, a term's place in it where its taxonomy has no address, and a taxonomy's key in a query; what a term's page is told of the taxonomy and the term it settles, with the settled taxonomy kept in its whole list's terms; and that a body's lists are evaluated once, at its top, in the order they are written, those in a site's components at any depth among them, a list in a component that renders nothing of its body in its place all the same.

## Decision

- **A post list** is the leaf directive `::post-list{…}` with no body. Its attributes, in this order: `type` and `presentation`, required; `include` and `exclude`, `<taxonomy>:<term>,<term>`, taxonomies apart by a space; `where`, conditions `<field><op><value>` apart by `;`, its op one of `=`, `!=`, `<`, `<=`, `>` and `>=`, a date's value a day, and `;` and `%` in a text as `%3B` and `%25`; `order` and `direction`; `offset`, 0, and `number`, 10, from 1 to 100; `leaveOutThis` and `leaveOutShown`, `true`; `pages`, `false`; and `empty`, the list's own text. The options of its presentation follow.
- **In the field,** a dialog is the query builder, and the canvas shows a sentence such as *10 articles in News and not in Sport, by title, from the 2nd, as Cards (Columns: 3).* Terms are kept by ID in source and written as slugs. One no longer there is left out with a warning, and a group left without terms with it.
- **The evaluator** is one pure function, free of Astro, run at every build: filter, order, leave out, then offset and number. *Related* ranks by a score of the terms each post shares with this one, times its freshness, and keeps only posts that score, as [ADR 0070](0070-rank-related-posts-by-shared-topics-and-category-times-a-freshness-that-halves.md) has it. Without `order`, a type with a date is newest first, else by title; ties fall to title, then slug; titles are compared in the site's language. A missing value orders last. A template may call it too, and a browser can run it later.
- **A body's lists are evaluated in the order they are written,** those in a site's components at any depth among them, once, by `Body` at the top of the body, before it renders any, so that `leaveOutShown` leaves out the posts of the list's type that the lists written above it showed, wherever each stands. A component's body is rendered from the parts the top read and evaluated. A list in a component's body is evaluated in its place whether the component renders its body, part of it or none of it, so the lists below leave out its posts even where the page does not show them.
- **Presentations** are declared under `presentations`, each with a `label` and typed options, and each is a component of the site's design, registered in `konzilo({ presentations })`. A declared presentation without a component, and one without a declaration, fail the build. The site template gives `list` and `cards`.
- **What a presentation is given.** The posts of its page, each `{ entry, url }`; its options; the text for an empty list; the pager, or null; and `list`, what the whole list holds over all its pages, after its offset and what it leaves out, in the page's language: `{ type: { name, label, plural }, total, terms: { <taxonomy>: [{ id, name, count, url }] }, search }`. `terms` holds each taxonomy the type takes, by its name, and the terms its posts hold, each by its ID, its slug, with its name, how many of the posts hold it, not counting those filed below it, and its page's address, most posts first, then by name. A term of a taxonomy without an address is there as any other, with `url` null, since a filter's row needs no page. `search` is the search page's address, null on a site without search and for a type whose posts are not in it. `listedPosts()` gives the same as the property `list` of the posts it returns.
- **The rows are the design's, the whole list the core's.** Which rows of terms a list that filters shows is an option the site declares for its presentation, since a row is the design's idea; what the whole list holds is the core's to give, since only the evaluator sees past the page shown. A taxonomy's key in a query, in a filter's row and on the search page, is its name, `category`, `topics` or `authors`.
- **Pages.** One list per post may have pages, at `<address>page/<n>/`: page 1 is the post's own address, and `page/1/` leads to it with `301`; from page 2, each page is `noindex` and canonical to itself, and is not in the sitemap. The integration's pager, in the page's language, has no style of its own. A second list with pages in a post is refused. Every address below `<address>page/` is the post's; the start page an Admin chose has its later pages at `/page/<n>/`, which its route draws ([ADR 0033](0033-keep-menus-settings-and-the-sites-identity-in-files-an-admin-edits.md)).
- **Term pages.** A taxonomy may declare `address`, such as `/category/{slug}/`, and `konzilo({ termPage })` names one page of the site's design for it. Each term's pages hold its posts, of every type that takes the taxonomy, filed under the term or a term below it, newest first, ten a page. They join the order of addresses as posts do. A taxonomy with an address and no term page fails the build. Beside the term, its page's posts, the pager and the text for an empty list, a term's page is told `taxonomy`, `{ name, label }`, the taxonomy's name, its key in a query, and its label; `settled`, `{ taxonomy, term }`, the taxonomy's name and the term's slug; and `list`, what its whole list holds, as a post list's presentation is given it, the same on each of its pages. Its `type` is null where several types take the taxonomy, its `terms` hold each taxonomy any of them takes, and its `search` is the search page's address where any of their posts are in the search.
- **Later dynamic lists** stay open, since the query is data and the evaluator pure.

## Why

- The query is data in the content file, and the design stays the site's.
- One evaluator keeps build time and a future browser in step.
- Every publish builds the site, so no list is ever stale.
- What a list shows follows from the body as written, so it is the same at every build and under every design. Astro renders the components of a body side by side, each as it gets to it, so a list evaluated where its component renders it would leave out what happened to be evaluated first. `postPages()` counts a list's pages from the body as written, before anything is rendered, so a list its component never renders must count there too, or a list with pages below it would have pages the count does not match.

## Considered and rejected

- **Templates written by editors.** The design would live in content.
- **Automatic term archives.** Thomas wants none.
- **Relative dates.** A list would go stale between publishes.
- **Evaluating a list where its component renders it.** The order would be Astro's, not the body's.
- **Leaving out a list its component never renders.** Whether a component renders its body is known only once it has rendered, after the lists below it must be evaluated; and a design changing how it draws a component would change what other lists show.
- **The rows as an attribute of the list.** A row is a design's idea, and a site already declares it as an option of its presentation, which the core passes on as it is.
- **Leaving the settled taxonomy out of a term's page's `terms`, or marking its term there.** `settled` already names both, so a presentation that filters leaves out that row by its name, and the one presentation draws a term's page and a list in a body alike; and in a hierarchical taxonomy the row still says how many posts each term below the settled one holds, which a design may show to narrow further.
- **`listedPosts()` returning `{ posts, list }`.** Every template that calls it would break, so the posts it returns carry `list` as a property.
- **Leaving a taxonomy without an address out of `terms`.** A filter's row and the search page need a term's ID and name, not its page.
