# ADR 0079: Mirror an incoming feed's items as published posts that only the feed reader changes

**Date:** 2026-11-26 · **Status:** Accepted. Settled by Thomas in triage on 2026-11-25: an Admin adds RSS 2.0 and Atom feeds, each read at an interval into a type of the Admin's choice, every five minutes by default, with a language on a site in several, and an Editor sees no feed's settings; each item a feed gives after it was added becomes a **published** post in the next build, so that a trigger waiting for it releases its post (ADR 0076), and a type whose posts cannot be published from what a feed gives cannot be chosen; the post is identical to its item and only the feed changes it: a correction follows, a withdrawal deletes it, and falling out of the feed is no withdrawal; no Editor changes, unpublishes or deletes it, its form opens read-only and says why, and the build refuses a change to it pushed by any account but the feed reader's; it takes nothing of the item's images; a withdrawn post is deleted, not purged, and listed on the *Deleted* page with no *Restore*; a feed's own items when it is added make no posts; removing a feed leaves its posts published, as posts like any other; and no service is named, reading feeds being an adapter an Admin turns on by adding a feed and off by removing every feed (ADR 0003). The rest is a technical choice; Thomas may veto, among them, chosen in building it on 2026-11-26:

- **Where the feeds are kept:** in the service's data, as the mail settings are ([ADR 0044](0044-take-visitors-requests-at-one-service-below-konzilo-and-keep-nothing-of-the-visitor.md)), by a step of their own: each feed with its address, its type, its language, its minutes between reads, when it was last read, whether its first read is taken, and its last failure; and each item it has given, by its ID in the feed, with when it was first read, its post's moment, whether it is `held`, given at the feed's first read and so no post, `live` or `withdrawn`, and its post's path, version and slug. Not in a settings group in Git, which every Editor can read, and which the feed reader, no Admin, could not write.
- **Who reads them:** the service in its role for editors, which alone has a way out and reaches Forgejo, as a timed duty at each minute that reads each feed whose interval has passed, one after another, and a feed just added at once, which takes its first read; `visitors` keeps no way out. With no feeds the duty reads nothing and reaches nothing. Not a program of its own, which Compose would run whether or not there were feeds.
- **The feed reader's account:** a machine account of its own, `feed-reader`, which the seed makes at whichever start finds it missing, enrolled with a second factor as every machine account is ([ADR 0007](0007-sign-in-through-the-customers-own-forgejo-with-two-factors-and-close-the-rest-at-the-proxy.md)), a collaborator who may write the source repository and in no team, so no person's role; its token, `write:repository`, is in `editors-secret`, `feed-token`, which only the role for editors mounts. A local copy's people may not take its name. Every read of a feed that changes posts is one commit through Forgejo's API, `Read the feed <address>`, and removing a feed one more, `Release the posts of the feed <address>`.
- **How a change is told:** the SHA-256 of the item's title, text, date and link as the feed gives them, kept in the post's source file, `feed.version`, and in the service's data; a read whose item's differs writes the post again from the item. An item is known by its RSS `guid` or Atom `id`, else its link.
- **What an item gives:** RSS's `title`, `link`, `guid`, `pubDate`, else Dublin Core's `date`, and `content:encoded`, else `description`; Atom's `title`, its alternate `link`, `id`, `published`, else `updated`, and `content`, else `summary`, as text, HTML or XHTML, one given by `src` left for the summary. A title is plain, its markup taken out; an item without one takes the start of its text, 80 characters at most, cut at a word; an item with neither an ID nor a link, or neither a title nor a text, is left out. A link is kept where it is a whole `http://` or `https://` address, relative ones read from the item's or the feed's base.
- **How HTML becomes the body:** read by parse5 into the content format's elements the post's type takes: paragraphs, headings from level 2, lists, quotes, code blocks, simple tables, and the inline formats, a link where it leads to a whole web address, `mailto:` or `tel:`, else its text. A heading, a list, a quote or a code block the type does not take becomes paragraphs of its text; a table it does not take, or one that is no simple table, goes whole; each is named in the source file, `feed.dropped`, and the intake warns of it. Images, media, frames, scripts and forms are never taken, and never warned of, since no item's image could be. The body is checked as the intake checks it, and made of the text's paragraphs alone where it would be refused all the same. A plain text is paragraphs, by its blank lines.
- **The post:** `title`, `slug`, made from the title by the editor's `slugFrom()` and kept by every correction, `status: published`, `date`, an article's main headline, `headline`, which it requires, as its title, `originalAddress`, the item's link, and `feed`, `{ item, version, dropped }`, which marks it as the feed's (`content-format.md`, *A post made from a feed*). Its date is the item's publishing moment, or when it was first read where the item gives none or one ahead. A type can be chosen where it takes paragraphs and requires no field but the main headline, so not one whose address holds a term, which is then required ([ADR 0080](0080-let-a-types-address-hold-its-posts-date-and-the-term-of-a-field-that-takes-one.md)). The feed reader fills no address: the intake gives the post its address from its slug and its date, so that a corrected date moves it only where its type's address holds the date, the address it was live at leading to the new. A post's ID is twelve hexadecimal digits, as the editor's `{{uuid_short}}`; on a site in several languages its file is in its feed's language's folder.
- **The address of the original** is `originalAddress` in the content file, a whole web address, which a template reads and which a post of any type may hold; it stays when the feed is removed, and Sveltia keeps it through an Editor's later saves, as it keeps a key it does not know.
- **A withdrawal** compares moments: a known item missing from a read is withdrawn where some item the read gives has a moment earlier than its, and has fallen out otherwise; an item whose moment the oldest item shares stays. A read of no items, a read that fails, and a feed's first read withdraw nothing. A withdrawn item that comes back is written again at its own path. A correction whose file is gone writes it again; a withdrawal whose file is gone changes nothing.
- **The limits:** a feed's answer of at most 5 MB, read whole within 30 seconds, redirects followed, asked for with no credential and nothing of the site's; an address `http://` or `https://`, which no other feed of the site has; an interval of whole minutes, from 1 to 1440. What cannot be read, answers otherwise than 2xx, is too large, takes too long, is no feed, has no type the site still has, or whose posts cannot be saved, is kept as the feed's failure by its kind, `unreachable`, `timeout`, `status` with the status, `too-large`, `not-a-feed`, `no-type` or `not-saved`, with its moment, tried again at the next interval, and changes nothing the service knows of the feed's items; the next read that succeeds clears it.
- **Changing a feed:** its type, its language and its interval change; its address does not, and another address is another feed, removed and added. Posts already made keep their type, and are corrected in it.
- **The *Feeds* page** is an Admin's alone, as the *Mail* page is: each feed as a group of its own, named by its address, with its choices, when it was last read and its last failure, and *Save* and *Remove*; and a group to add one, whose type is chosen among those a feed may become. *Remove* asks first, in the browser's own dialog, saying that the feed's posts stay published, as posts like any other. The service releases them before it forgets the feed, and keeps the feed where they could not be saved.
- **The *Publishing* page** lists each feed that could not be read, by its address, with the moment and the reason, to everyone who may write, until a read succeeds.
- **The read-only form** is the lock's, as an Admin's form is to an Editor ([ADR 0073](0073-lock-a-form-to-the-tab-that-has-it-open-and-refuse-a-save-older-than-the-latest.md)): the service answers a hold of a feed's post's form, whoever asks, that it is a feed's, by the posts the service knows it made, and the form opens read-only, with no takeover, no Save and no Delete, saying that the post is made from a feed and only the feed changes it; a deletion from the list is refused in the same words. A feed's post the *Deleted* page lists, its last revision marked as the feed's, says that only its feed brings it back, and offers no *Restore*. The editor writes no `feed` into a file it saves, so that a copy of a feed's post, made by Sveltia's *Duplicate*, is a post like any other, and keeps the address of the original.
- **The build's check** goes by who pushed, as a Reviewer's does ([ADR 0008](0008-give-customers-three-roles-admin-editor-and-reviewer.md)): a post whose source file says it is a feed's, or whose last accepted version did, which the ledger keeps as `fromFeed`, and which anyone but the feed reader was the last to change, and is not back at what was last accepted, keeps the version last published, naming who changed it, `refusal.feed-post`; one whose file anyone else took away stays on the site, `refusal.feed-post-gone`. A developer's own checkout, which holds nothing to a role, holds nothing to the feed reader either.

## Decision

- **An Admin's feeds:** RSS 2.0 and Atom feeds of other sites, each with its address, the type its items become, its interval and, on a site in several languages, its language, which only an Admin sees and changes.
- **An item becomes a published post** at the next read after it appears, with its title, its text as the type's body elements, its date and a link to the original, a slug made from its title; the build publishes it as any published post.
- **The post mirrors its item.** A correction under the same ID changes the post, its slug kept; a withdrawal deletes it; an item that fell out of a feed that keeps only its latest items leaves its post as it is.
- **Only the feed changes it.** Its form is read-only for everyone, and the build refuses a change to it by any account but the feed reader's.
- **A feed added publishes nothing of its backlog,** and a feed removed leaves its posts published, as posts like any other.
- **A feed that cannot be read** says so on the *Publishing* page until it can.

## Why

- A press release service, or a newsroom of the customer's own, publishes the same text in more than one place; a site that mirrors it says the same as the original, at once, and nobody copies it by hand.
- A mirrored post an Editor could change would no longer be the item, and a correction from the feed would overwrite the change or have to be merged; the feed is the one source.
- A withdrawal must follow, since a sensitive item published by mistake is withdrawn quickly, but a feed of its latest items drops old ones every day, which must not empty the site.
- The feed reader's own account makes every change it makes show in the History and the role check as its own, so that the check by who pushed holds outside the editor too.

## Considered and rejected

- **Drafts from a feed, for an Editor to publish.** Settled otherwise: a post goes out at once, and a trigger can wait for it.
- **Telling a withdrawal by a missing item alone.** A feed of its latest items would delete every post that fell out.
- **Taking an item's images.** They come without alt text, which no image on the site goes without ([ADR 0022](0022-make-each-images-master-from-its-original-and-write-images-and-galleries-in-markdown.md)).
- **The editor's own read-only check of an entry's data.** The service knows which posts it made, and answers the lock's hold, which every form asks before it may save or delete.

## Consequences

- `content-format.md` describes a post made from a feed, `originalAddress` and the ledger's `fromFeed`, and the site description refuses a field named `feed` or `originalAddress`.
- [ADR 0008](0008-give-customers-three-roles-admin-editor-and-reviewer.md) holds a feed's post to the feed reader as it holds a Reviewer's change, and [ADR 0044](0044-take-visitors-requests-at-one-service-below-konzilo-and-keep-nothing-of-the-visitor.md) has the feed reader among its timed duties.
- `GLOSSARY.md` defines an incoming feed, the feed reader and a post made from a feed.
