# ADR 0080: Let a type's address hold its post's date, and the term of a field that takes one

**Date:** 2026-11-25 · **Status:** Accepted. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-25, in triage: a type's address may hold `{year}`, `{year}/{month}` or `{year}/{month}/{day}`, together and in that order, the month and the day in two digits, from the post's date in UTC, as every date the site shows; and the term of a field of terms that takes exactly one, `"most": 1` and `"required": true`, by the field's name, its path in a hierarchical taxonomy; each token fills one or more whole steps, appears at most once and stands before the final `{slug}`, and no text shares a step with it; a term and `{path}` are never combined, while a type that is not hierarchical may combine a term and a date; a draft and a scheduled post are at the address their date gives as it stands, and the live preview and the form show it, and a post a trigger releases is dated, and so addressed, as it goes out; a changed date, term or term slug, or a term moved under another parent, moves the post, the address it was live at an automatic `301` ([ADR 0036](0036-keep-every-redirect-as-a-rule-and-make-old-addresses-from-what-was-live.md)); two types, or a type and a taxonomy, whose addresses could meet are told apart as `{slug}` and `{path}` are, a date step matching any one step and a hierarchical taxonomy's term any number; not offered: a *main term* among several, a field that takes several terms, a post's ID, a site's own fields that are not terms, a time zone of the site's own, and pages for a year, a month or a day. The rest is a technical choice; Thomas may veto, among them, chosen in building it on 2026-11-25:

- **The built-in article's category,** which takes one at most and which a site cannot declare again, is required where the article's address holds `{category}`, the form, the intake and the reader taking it so, since the address is what makes it required. Its authors and topics, which take several, are refused there, as any other field that does not take exactly one.
- **Two addresses of one shape are refused,** as two that are the same are: each text as it is, each token that fills one step alike, and each that may fill several alike, so that `/{year}/{slug}/` and `/{category}/{slug}/` of a category that is not hierarchical are refused, since every address one gives the other gives too. Two that can only meet, as `/{year}/{slug}/` and `/2026/{slug}/`, or `/{year}/{month}/{day}/{slug}/` and `/{path}/`, are left to their posts, as `/{slug}/` and `/{path}/` are: the intake refuses the one post that would take an address another holds. A type's address that can give the search page's, through a token too, is refused, as before.
- **A post's file stays at its slug,** or its path, whatever else its address holds, so that the files, the ledger's paths and Astro's entries are what they were, and a type's two posts never share a slug, even where their dates or terms would give them different addresses.
- **A date token needs a date:** a type of the site's own without `"date": {}` among its fields is refused one, naming the field to declare. A date token on a hierarchical type is not refused, since the triage excluded only a term there.
- **A post's date in its address** is the date its file has: its own, the moment its triggers came to hold, else the moment the intake first took it; in a live preview a draft without one is where it would be from now.
- **One place fills an address,** `filledAddress()` of `format/addresses.js`, and `publicAddress()` takes a post's values, `{ name, date, terms }`, where it took its slug or path. A content file gives them, `contentValues()`, from its frontmatter, its date and its terms' slugs, and from the term files a hierarchical term's parents, which the Astro side reads from the content folder as it stands, each file again once it has changed, so that the dev server follows a term moved or renamed; the intake gives them from the source.
- **Breadcrumbs** find the post at a shorter address by the slug or path its type's address gives there, and take it as a level only where its own values give it that address.
- **The form's address,** the slug's link and the canonical address's placeholder, is filled in the browser from the form's slug, date and term as they stand, a post without a date from now, and reads a term's slug and parents from the source repository's term files, the main language's on a site in several, through Forgejo with the editor's credential, each file taken as read for half a minute. The live preview shows the address the intake gives the form's values, as before; the publish status shows the address the release shows the post at.

## Decision

- **The checker** (`site-description.js`, `typePath()`) reads a type's address once its fields are, and refuses, each naming the type with an example that works: text sharing a step with a token, a token twice, `{slug}` or `{path}` before the last step, the date's tokens out of order or apart, a date token on a type without a date, a token that names no field, a field that holds no term, a field of terms that does not take exactly one, and a term on a hierarchical type. A taxonomy's address keeps `{slug}` and `{path}` alone.
- **Meeting addresses:** `claim()` refuses an address of the same shape as another type's or taxonomy's in a language, which `shapeOf()` gives; `stepsGive()` says whether an address can give another, for the search page.
- **Every caller** gets a post's address from `publicAddress()` with the post's values: the intake's places, old addresses and the address a refused post wants, the redirect rules, search, the demo source, every page, list, link, sitemap, feed, head, alternate and breadcrumb of the Astro side, and the form.
- **The ledger and the old addresses** need nothing new: the ledger records each address a post is live at, and a move by its date, term or term slug leaves the last as an old address, as a changed slug does.

## Why

- Breadcrumbs follow a post's address and nothing else ([ADR 0037](0037-give-hierarchical-types-addresses-that-follow-their-parents-and-derive-breadcrumbs.md)), so the address is where a site says where a post stands; posts made in numbers stand by their category or their date, not by a place an editor gives each.
- A term that takes exactly one, and a date every post has, give an address with no empty step and no choice among several.
- UTC is the zone of every date the site shows, so an address and the date its page shows never name different days.
- A file at its slug keeps every reader of the content files, and every file another tool writes, as it was; a site that wants one slug twice in a type is rare.

## Considered and rejected

- **A post's file at its address's steps,** `articles/2026/03/07/lanterns.md`, which would let the Astro side read an address from the file's name alone. Every reader of an entry's ID as a slug would change, and a changed date or term would move the file as well as the address.
- **The address written into the content file.** It is derived, and another tool that writes the files would have to derive it too.
- **Refusing every two addresses that could meet.** `/{year}/{month}/{day}/{slug}/` beside pages at `/{path}/` is the commonest shape of all, and its posts never meet in practice; the intake refuses the one that would.
