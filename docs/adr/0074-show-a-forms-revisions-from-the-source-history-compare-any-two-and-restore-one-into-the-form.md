# ADR 0074: Show a form's revisions from the source history, compare any two, and restore one into the form

**Date:** 2026-11-23 · **Status:** Accepted. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-22 and 2026-11-23: an Editor sees every **revision** of a form, compares any two field by field, and restores one into the form as unsaved changes; nothing new is stored. The rest is a technical choice; Thomas may veto, among them, chosen in building it on 2026-11-23:

- **Who reads the history:** the service's role for editors, at `/_konzilo/revisions?path=<file>&page=<n>` on the admin host, with its own credential that reads the source, to whoever may write, which is whoever may open a form. It lists the commits that changed the file through Forgejo's API, twenty at a time, newest first, and gives each with the file as that commit left it, so that a page of the list needs one call, and a commit no longer in the history, as after a purge, is in no answer. Nothing is kept of a file's text.
- **The account and the moment of a revision** are those of the push that brought its commit, from the repository's activity feed, as the builder learns them for the role check (ADR 0008), each push's commits listed by Forgejo's API. A private account's pushes the feed shows only to those who run Forgejo, so the service reads it by a token of the instance's account that can do nothing but read, as the builder does, which the seed makes at a start that finds it missing; the service keeps what it has learned in memory alone, and asks the feed only for what is new. Forgejo records a push in the feed a moment after it, so the push of a commit made within the last two minutes is waited for, ten seconds at most. A commit the feed has no push of says that Forgejo has no record of its account, and its moment is the commit's own.
- **The service's token of the instance's account,** `pushes-token` in its secrets, which the seed makes with the scope `read:repository` alone, is the one credential of the service's role for editors that reaches beyond the source repository: the instance's account runs Forgejo, so the token reads every repository on the instance, its files, commits and activity feed, private accounts' pushes among them, and can change nothing. It is chosen since the feed hides a private account's pushes from the service's own reader, which reads the source repository alone, and naming the account that pushed a revision is what makes the history trustworthy (ADR 0008); the builder reads the same feed by a token of that account for its role check. A token of an account that reads the source alone would name no private account's push. The service uses it for the feed and a push's commits only, and keeps it in its secrets as it keeps its other credentials.
- **The History** is the panel beside the form where Sveltia listed the file's commits by the author each commit names, which the core's own list replaces. A new entry has none until its first save, as it has no lock.
- **A revision is read** through Sveltia's own reading of a file for the form's collection, which takes the format the form's file is read with, the core's flat format for a post among them, into the flat map of values Sveltia holds a form's in; the patched Sveltia hands the core that, with its own restore of a draft backup, its check of unsaved changes and its backup of them.
- **The comparison** is a dialog over the page, with two lists to choose each side from: the form now, the changes a restore replaced, the revisions the panel has read, and, from the refusal, the latest save. A field's kind follows its widget: text marked word by word, the longest run of words the two share kept; a list's items, an item in both but out of the order they share moved; an image by its small picture and its address; anything else, a relation by its entry's name among them, shown before and after. A field alike on both sides is listed under one fold that says how many. A value no field holds today is named by its key, with what it held on each side.
- **The body** is compared block by block, by the reader the field's early check uses: two blocks the same in everything but their place are alike; a removed and an added block in the same place, of one kind or with the same words, are one changed block, by its words where they differ, marked word by word, and otherwise in form, shown as its Markdown both ways.
- **A word marked** is `del` or `ins`, each with words a screen reader reads and the page does not show, *Removed:* or *Added:*, and a moved item *Moved:*.
- **Restoring** takes the values of every field the form has today from the revision, and for a post keeps its own status and its date as they are, the fields `status` and `date` at the top of its file, while a field so named within another is restored as any field; a value no field holds stays as the form has it. Where the form holds unsaved changes, the Editor is asked first; those changes are then written as the draft backup, and kept at the top of the form's history for as long as the page is open, to compare and restore, since the backup follows the form from its next change on. The restore is offered only in a form that is not read-only, which is a form whose lock the tab holds, or one the service cannot be asked about, as a save is.
- **The refusal of a save older than the latest** gets a button below its words, *Compare the latest save with your changes*, which opens the comparison with the latest save on one side, as the history has it, named by the account that pushed it, and the form on the other.

## Decision

- **Every form has its revisions:** a post's, a translation's as a post of its own, a term's, an image library entry's, the site settings' and each menu's in each language, as every form has a lock ([ADR 0073](0073-lock-a-form-to-the-tab-that-has-it-open-and-refuse-a-save-older-than-the-latest.md)). Whoever may open a form sees its revisions. Only whoever may save it restores one.
- **Read from the source repository's history,** with nothing stored beside it. A revision is each commit that changed the form's file, listed newest first, with its moment, the account that pushed it as Forgejo recorded it, not as the commit says ([ADR 0008](0008-give-customers-three-roles-admin-editor-and-reviewer.md)), and the status it left a post in. What is purged is gone from every revision, as it is from the history.
- **Any two can be compared,** the form's unsaved changes as either side too: field by field, under the form's own labels, with fields alike folded away. Text is marked word by word; an image or a file is shown before and after; a list's items added, removed or moved are marked. A field the form no longer has is named, with what it held.
- **The body is compared as it reads,** block by block and word by word. A block whose words are alike but whose formatting or link targets changed is marked as changed in form, and shown both ways.
- **Restoring fills the form** with the revision's fields as unsaved changes, for the Editor to see before saving. It needs the lock. Where the form holds unsaved changes, the Editor is asked first whether to replace them, and what is replaced stays in the draft backup. Only the fields the form has today are restored; the status and the moment stay as they are, so restoring never publishes or unpublishes by itself. The slug is restored as any field, and a published post's address then moves, with the automatic old address that every move gets ([ADR 0036](0036-keep-every-redirect-as-a-rule-and-make-old-addresses-from-what-was-live.md)). A value that fails today's checks, such as a term since removed, is refused at Save as any value is.
- **Saving a restored revision makes a new one;** nothing in the history is removed or rewritten.
- **A refusal of a save older than the latest** (ADR 0073) offers to compare the latest save with the form's unsaved changes.

## Why

- An Editor who has changed a text, or finds it changed by someone else, needs to see what it said and to get it back without asking Kntnt. The source repository has held every revision all along, but only someone who works in Git could reach it.
- Formatting and link targets are compared too, since a link that now leads elsewhere can matter more than a word, and a word-only comparison would not show it.
- Restoring as unsaved changes lets the Editor see what will change, and keeps restoring one save like any other, with the lock, the checks and the status rules it already has.

## Considered and rejected

- **A store of revisions of Konzilo's own.** The history already holds every save, and a second copy would have to be purged and moved with it.
- **Comparing the body's words alone.** A changed link target would not show.
- **Full tracked changes in the body, as a word processor shows them.** Far more work for little more than the marked blocks give.
- **Restoring the status and the moment too.** Restoring would then publish or unpublish by itself.

## Consequences

- Restoring a deleted entry, which has no form, is not part of this decision.
- `GLOSSARY.md` defines a revision.
