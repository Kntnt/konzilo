# ADR 0045: Let a developer make each form, and an editor choose among them

**Date:** 2026-11-02, amended 2026-11-11, 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13 · **Status:** Accepted, settled by Thomas on 2026-11-02: editors do not build forms. A developer makes each form as a component, simple or advanced, an editor chooses among them and fills in what the form allows, and a new one is ordered. The rest is a technical choice; Thomas may veto. Settled on 2026-11-11, when the service first took forms, as technical choices Thomas may veto: a form sent without JavaScript is taken without a timing token; a token is signed, too soon under three seconds and refused after a day; the trap field and a token too soon are answered as sent and held as spam; a token missing or not the service's, a field the form does not declare, a form the site does not declare and a page not the site's own are refused; ten sends from one client in a window of ten minutes, spam among them; an email address with a dot in its domain; and a submission keeps no address of the page it came from. Settled on 2026-11-11, when Admins and Editors first read the submissions, as technical choices Thomas may veto: the *Submissions* page only on a site that declares a form, each form in the site's order and then any other whose submissions are kept, by its name, fifty at a time, newest first; the moments in the reader's own time zone, the browser's, on the page and in the file alike, since a site has no time zone of its own, and UTC where the browser names none the service knows, the zone named by its IANA name in the label beside each moment on the page and in the file's first column's heading, *Sent at (Europe/Stockholm)*, rather than by an offset in each value, which a spreadsheet would read as text and not as a moment; the file's column names and a box's words, *Yes* or *No*, in the reader's interface language, the boxes ticked joined by a comma and a space, the form by its label, or by its name once the site no longer declares it, and a column for each of two fields that share a label; a cell in quotes where it holds the separator chosen, a quote or a line break; and the file fetched with the editor's credential and saved by the browser as `<form>-<day>.csv`, since the credential is in the browser and no cookie carries it. Settled on 2026-11-12, when submissions were first mailed, as technical choices Thomas may veto: each submission but spam is mailed once, and one whose turn comes while mail is off, or while its form has no recipients, is mailed to no one, then or later, so that mail turned on sends nothing kept before it; a form's recipients are up to 50 addresses, each with a dot in its domain, as a visitor's must have, written one per line on the *Mail* page; the notification is plain text in the page's language, its subject *New submission:* and the form's label, then a line naming the form, each field's label and value on lines of their own, as the *Submissions* page shows them, and a line saying that every submission is on that page; it goes from the sender's address the Admin names to all the form's recipients in one message, dated when the submission was sent, answering to the address in the form's first email field where the visitor gave one, marked as sent by a program, so that an out-of-office reply leaves it alone, with no link, since the service knows no address of the editor's to give. Settled on 2026-11-12, when submissions were first kept for their form's retention, and a person's found, exported and erased, as technical choices Thomas may veto: a form's retention is a whole number of days from 1 to 3650, 30 until an Admin sets another, beside its recipients on the *Mail* page, where each form is a group of its own, rather than in the forms settings group in Git; a form the site no longer declares keeps its submissions for the retention it had; spam is kept 7 days, or its form's retention where that is shorter; a person's submissions are on a page of their own, *Personal data*, an Admin's alone, rather than on the *Submissions* page an Editor reads too, found by a text that a value written in a submission holds, in any case and any form of a letter, never by a field's label, in every form, the forms the site no longer declares and spam among them, newest first, a hundred at most, the page saying where there are more; each one found is ticked until the Admin unticks one that is not the person's, and those ticked download as CSV, as a form's do, each row with its own form's label, saved as `personal-data-<day>.csv`, or are erased once the Admin has said for good. Settled on 2026-11-12 too, as a technical choice Thomas may veto: removing a person's submissions at their request is *erasure*, the word privacy law uses for it, and removing a post and its media from every repository's history is a *purge*, so that each word has one meaning in `GLOSSARY.md`. Settled on 2026-11-12, when a field's label first held a link, as technical choices Thomas may veto: a field's label may hold links wherever the form draws it, in a `label` or in a group's `legend`, and an option's label may not, since an option's words are the value a submission keeps; a link opens where any link does, in the same tab; and a label's link that leads nowhere a menu's entry may lead is a problem of the site description, named by its path. Settled on 2026-11-12, when a form first had a button text of its own, as technical choices Thomas may veto: a form's declaration gives it as `button`, one text, or on a site in several languages one by locale, one for each, as a type's address may be, and without it the button says the site's word for sending; the declaration lists under `editable` the texts an editor may change where the form is placed, `button` alone, since a placeholder's and a label's text wait until a site asks for them, and the description refuses `editable` naming any other; an editor gives the text as the form directive's attribute `button`, after `name`, any text but white space, in the post's language, each translation being a post with a placement of its own, so that one set per placement and per language needs nothing more; the intake, and the body field's check in the browser, refuse the attribute where the form does not list it, naming the form, by the rule data's `editable` on the attribute and what the site lets editors change beside the names it declares, so that one check serves both; a post's last file whose text its form no longer allows shows the form's own text, without a warning, since the developer took that text from editors and the form itself is still declared, and a template that gives one is read the same way; and the structure editor's box for a form shows a field for the button's text where the chosen form allows it, empty for the form's own, and drops the text when the editor chooses a form that does not.

## Decision

- **Written** as `::form{name="contact"}` (ADR 0019). Each attribute is declared, with a type, a default and the values it allows. The rule data's `declared` says that `name` must be a form the site declares; the refusal names what it declares.
- **A simple form** is a list of fields in `site-description.json`, drawn by the core's component.
- **An advanced form** is a component of the site's own, with any HTML, CSS and JavaScript: conditions, progressive disclosure, prefilled values. Its scripts are bundled, so the Content Security Policy holds. It still declares its fields, and posts to the same place.
- **What an editor may change** is what the form declares, such as a placeholder or a button's text, written as attributes, one set per placement and per language. A form declares the texts an editor may change under `editable`, and its own button text as `button`; the button's text is built, `::form{name="contact" button="Book the assessment"}`, and the intake refuses it where the form does not list it. The confirmation text is the Admin's setting, and the recipients and the retention the Admin's on the *Mail* page, kept in the service.
- **One way in for every form.** Each sends to `/_konzilo/forms/<form>`, so spam protection, storage, mail, retention and CSV are built once. The service checks known fields, their kinds and lengths. A condition such as a field being required only when another is filled in is checked in the browser, until a form needs it on the server too. Its checks are the page's script's, and an email address must also have a dot in its domain, as every address mail reaches has; the script names that beside the field when the service refuses it.
- **What the service answers.** To the page's script, JSON with an outcome, which the page shows; to a browser without JavaScript, `303` back to the page the form gives in `_page`, or to the site's root for what is no path of the site's, with the outcome's id in the fragment. A field wrong is `invalid` with each wrong field's problem. Too many sends is `too-many`, with `Retry-After` and the seconds in the answer. A token missing or not the service's, a field the form does not declare, a form the site does not declare, a body that is no form, and a request from a page not the site's own (ADR 0044) are `refused`, which asks the visitor to load the page again, since only a changed form or another site's page gives them. The trap field filled in, and a send too soon after its token, are answered `sent` and held as spam, so a robot learns nothing.
- **The timing token** is `<seconds>.<nonce>.<signature>`: when it was given, sixteen random characters, and HMAC-SHA256 of the form, the moment and the nonce under a key derived from the service's key. It is checked, not stored, and may be sent again. A send under three seconds after it is held as spam; a token older than a day is refused. The page's script must send one. A browser without JavaScript cannot ask for one, and no static page can hold one, so its post is taken without, its token checked where it gives one: the token stops a robot that runs scripts, and the trap field and the count stop the rest.
- **Ten sends from one client in a window of ten minutes,** by the digest of ADR 0044, spam among them; a send refused, or with a field wrong, is none.
- **A site may declare components of its own,** forms and others, each with its attributes. Attributes are data, never code. `preSave` and the intake refuse an unknown component or attribute.
- **A field's label may hold links,** written as Markdown links, `I have read the [privacy policy](/privacy-policy/)`, and nothing else of Markdown, each to an address a menu's entry may have: a path of the site's own, or one that starts with `https://`, `http://`, `mailto:` or `tel:`. The site description gives the label's words without the links, which a submission keeps and its mail, its CSV and the *Submissions* page show, and its parts, which the form draws, each link an `a` inside the field's `label` or `legend`, a link to a path of the site's own below the site's base, as a body's link is, so that a preview's stays in the preview (2026-11-13). An option's label holds none, since its words are the value sent.
- **The markup is the core's.** `Form.astro` renders a declared form: a plain HTML form, every field labelled, its help tied to it, the trap field hidden, and each outcome at the id the service's redirect names. A site restyles it, or replaces it with its own markup that keeps the core's classes, ids and data.
- **The script is part of the integration's page script file.** It asks for the timing token once the form comes into use, checks the fields as the service does, sends JSON, and shows the outcome in place with the focus on it. Without JavaScript the form posts, and is led back with the outcome in the fragment. The page holds no inline code.
- **The texts are the core's,** in English and Swedish; a site gives its own through `konzilo({ formTexts })`.
- **A page of the release stands in for the service's answer.** Every build writes `_konzilo/form-not-sent.html`, a plain page in the site's language offering the publisher's email address. While the service does not answer, the web server gives a post below `/_konzilo/forms/` that page with the same status. It is the one page below the prefix the web server serves, and only then; the other files there are the consent scripts (ADR 0049).
- **A preview shows the form and sends nothing.**
- **A form taken out of the description refuses the posts that place it,** naming the form, and they keep their last file, which the reader shows without the form, with a warning. Every other post publishes as before. A deploy can invalidate files already published, so the build checks too.
- **Submissions** are kept in the service for each form's retention, the days an Admin sets beside its recipients, 30 until then, read by Admins and Editors, and mailed to the form's recipients; a spam submission is mailed to no one and kept 7 days, or its form's retention where that is shorter. The service forgets each once its time has passed (ADR 0044). A submission keeps its fields with their labels as they came, so a form that changes keeps the submissions it had. It records the page's language, and its notification's words are in that language. It is the form, the moment, the language tag of the page's prefix, or the site's language, why it was held as spam where it was, and each field's name, label and value, a text, a box ticked or not, or the boxes ticked; not the page's address, which is the visitor's way through the site.
- **Reading them.** Admins and Editors read a form's submissions on the *Submissions* page in the editor, on a site that declares a form: a form chosen among the site's, and any other whose submissions are still kept, newest first, fifty at a time, each with its moment and its fields as they came, spam left out. The service in its role for editors reads them, and tells whoever may not write nothing, not even which forms there are (ADR 0044).
- **A person's request.** An Admin finds a person's submissions on the *Personal data* page, as privacy law asks, by what the person wrote in them, such as their email address or their name, in every form, spam among them, and downloads those that are theirs as CSV, as a form's are, or erases them. An erased submission is in no page or file after, and nothing of it stays in the service's data (ADR 0044).
- **Download.** A form's submissions download as CSV from the *Submissions* page, and a person's from the *Personal data* page, each row's *Form* its own: UTF-8 with a byte-order mark, rows ended by CRLF, written as they are read and never stored, its columns and its rows read from one moment's data, so that a submission kept while it is written is in neither; where the store fails before the first byte, the answer is an error, and after it the answer ends unfinished, so that the browser reports a failed download rather than keep a file cut short; commas or semicolons, as the user chooses, offering semicolons where the user's language writes a decimal comma. Columns: *Sent at*, `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS` in the reader's time zone, as the page shows each moment, which its heading names, *Sent at (Europe/Stockholm)*; *Form*, the form's label; then each field label, the newest submission's in its order, then each label only older ones hold, a column for each of two fields that share one. The column names, and a box ticked or not, *Yes* or *No*, are in the reader's interface language. A form's download leaves spam out, as the *Submissions* page does; a person's holds every submission the Admin ticked, spam among them, since privacy law asks for all that is theirs. **Safe to open:** a value that begins with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return is written after an apostrophe, and so is such a character, or a quote, after a comma, a semicolon or a line break inside a value.

## Why

- Forms range from a few fields to conditions and prefilled values, and no builder Konzilo could afford reaches the far end. A component reaches both.
- Markup in the core lets a fix to labels, ids or accessibility reach every site.
- A browser without JavaScript can show only what the host answers, and a page built with the release is in the site's language without the server knowing it.
- A name alone keeps a post's file free of what the developer may change, and every recipient out of the content files.

## Considered and rejected

- **A form builder for editors,** in the body or as entries, as form plugins for WordPress give.
- **A link of its own on a field,** a key beside the label. An acceptance line names its policy in the middle of a sentence, which the sentence's own words place and a language orders its own way.
- **Any inline Markdown in a label.** Emphasis and code in a label are styling a design gives, and a submission would have to strip each.
- **Links in an option's label.** An option's words are the value a submission keeps and a select's `option` cannot hold a link.
- **A link that opens in a tab of its own.** The visitor chooses that, and a link that does it unasked loses the form's place for those who use the back button.
- **The whole component in the site template.** Each copy would miss every later fix.
- **A redirect back to the form's page from the web server.** It cannot read the page from the body, and the `Referer` is not always there.
- **Tab-separated UTF-16,** which Excel opens with its columns in every region. It is not CSV, and other tools read it worse.
- **One separator chosen by the site's language alone.** An English-speaking editor of a Swedish site would get a file their Excel cannot split.
- **Excel's `sep=` line.** It makes Excel ignore the byte-order mark, and Swedish characters break.
- **An `.xlsx` workbook.** It needs a library.
- **Quoting every cell against the other separator.** A reader heeds a quote only where a cell of its own reading begins.
- **An offset in each moment of the file,** `2026-11-11 11:00:00 +01:00`. Excel and LibreOffice read it as text, which cannot be sorted or reckoned with as a moment; one heading names the zone of the whole column.

## What practice has shown

Reading a comma-separated file by semicolons, Python's `csv` in its Excel dialect begins a cell with `=` after `x;=1+1;` inside a quoted value, and LibreOffice begins a row after a line break inside one and evaluates the formula after it. With the apostrophe rule, reading each file by both separators with formulas evaluated evaluated none.
