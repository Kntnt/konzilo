# ADR 0049: Manage consent with Konzilo's own client, from one service registry

**Date:** 2026-10-29, amended 2026-11-10, 2026-11-12, 2026-11-25 and 2026-11-26 · **Status:** Accepted. Settled by Thomas on 2026-10-29: the GDPR and ePrivacy to the letter, deliberate departures recorded as such, only the EU's rules, and Real Cookie Banner as the yardstick. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-10: counting before consent only what a web server's log would hold, the right to object offered in the browser wherever counting without storage is, as one explanation whose own word is the link, and nothing of it while the visitor allows statistics, and plainer words in the dialog and the declaration. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-25: where a provider's documents disagree on a transfer, the facts follow the one that declares it, so Datawrapper's list a transfer to the USA with no safeguard its provider documents; and the banner's default is written from the facts of the services that ask for consent, an Admin's own text shown instead. Settled in triage on 2026-11-25: an editor places the right to object in a body by a leaf directive of the core's, which a type takes by listing it among its elements. A technical choice Thomas may veto (2026-11-26): the directive's name, `consent-objection`, the element's too; that the site template's page lists it, since a privacy policy is a page, and its article and canvas do not; that the integration draws it itself and refuses a site's own component for it, as it does the post list; that the intake warns of it by the site description's departure alone, so that a site with the departure whose settings choose no tracker for it has the departure's own warning instead; that where it draws nothing the live preview still gives its block a mark, on a wrapper with no box, as for any component that renders nothing; and that a page's Markdown alternate leaves out the right to object wherever the page holds it, a template's and the cookie declaration's too, whose link leads nowhere outside the page. Settled on 2026-11-26, when the records were first kept, as technical choices Thomas may veto: a record's time is the visitor's clock's, as the client sends it, taken up to a day ahead of the service's and back to 13 months, and the same consent ID and time sent again are stored once; a record's language must be one its revision's pages were shown in; the service answers `204` once a record is stored, which is all the client waits for, `400` for a record it refuses by its shape, its revision, its states or its language, `403` for one from another page, and `429`, with `Retry-After` until the hour or the client's window ends, for one counted and not stored, so that the client sends it once more at a later page view; every record taken counts toward both limits, stored or not, counted in the role's memory by the clock's own hour and the client's window of ADR 0044, and those not stored are counted by the day, in UTC, they came, which the Admin's page shows beside the choices and the duty forgets with the records; what a revision showed is written by each public build, beside its pages, as `_consent.json`, which the release keeps beside its pages as `consent.json`: the revision, each category's services, and, in each of the site's languages, the banner's texts, the privacy settings' texts with each category's name, description and services, each service's name, purpose, why it is necessary where it is and its provider with the provider's country, each placeholder's words, the right to object's explanation where it is offered, and the legal pages linked; the service keeps each language's words as a version of their own, from the moment the release that showed them went live, where they are not the words the revision's last version holds, so that an Admin's change of the banner's words, which changes no revision, is kept too, and shows a record with the version live at its time, or with the first one for a record made, by a visitor's clock behind the site's, before any; 13 months before a day that a shorter month lacks is that month's last day, so that 13 months before 31 December is 30 November; and the Admin's page, *Consent records*, in Swedish *Samtycken*, on every site, opens on the last 30 days, newest first, each day with how many choices each control made, *Accept all*, *Reject optional*, *Save my choices*, *Allow one service* and *Withdraw consent*, and how many were counted, not stored, takes a span of at most 400 days, finds a consent ID however it is copied, in capitals or with space around it, and exports a span as CSV with a column for the consent ID, the time, the revision, the language, the page, the part and the control, each of the four categories, and the services allowed and refused by their ids.

## Decision

- **Categories:** necessary, functional, statistics and marketing.
- **The service registry** is one JSON content file, `consent.json`, which only the intake writes, at every build, and Astro reads as the collection `consent`. It holds the consent's own storage, a local storage item kept 12 months, and with a shared domain a cookie on it too; the embed services the published posts use, from core facts that each name a source in the provider's own documentation and the day it was checked, a provider that uses what it learns for its own advertising or profiling, or whose player stores what another uses so, as Screen9's does Google's ad cookies, making its service marketing, any other functional; the services core features add; and the services an Admin declares in `services.json`, each with its id made from its name once and kept, so that a departure can name it. One the site cannot publish keeps the last registry. Each service has its provider, purpose, storage, retention, hosts per kind of request, and transfers out of the EU and EEA, each with the safeguards its provider documents for it, none where it documents none. Where a provider's documents disagree on a transfer, the facts list it, and its source's `note` says how they disagree: a declaration that says too much is safer than one that says too little. Datawrapper's privacy policy keeps published charts in AWS's region in Virginia and names no safeguard for it, while its page on embed privacy says that user data stays in the EEA, so its facts list a transfer to the USA with none. Each stored item names the domain it is on: the host of the page that stores it, a domain shared with the sites below it, or its provider's, the host its service shows in or loads from where the facts name none. The dialog and the cookie declaration show it.
- **Hosts reach the policy only through a service.** A host an Admin declares is an `https://` origin, `*.` allowed for subdomains, never a bare `*`, a scheme alone, a path, a query or a port, nor one of the site's own hosts. A `*.` never takes in a public suffix or shared domain of the Public Suffix List, or a name above one. The list is pinned in `versions.env` at a commit, and raised with each release.
- **The consent revision** is a hash of the categories, the services and the departures with their reasons, not the wording. A new revision asks every visitor again. The same inputs give the same bytes.
- **Konzilo's own client.** Pure functions render the banner, a native `<dialog>`, the footer controls, the placeholders and the cookie declaration, each an escaped HTML string from the registry, the consent texts, the legal pages, the page's language and the core's catalogues, whose texts named `site.consent.…` are the core's own words (`src/consent/markup.js`, ADR 0066). Astro's components are thin wrappers around them, and a page Konzilo does not build can call them. One script holds the signal (`src/consent/client.js`), with no library and no inline code. `src/consent/` imports nothing outside itself, so another site can copy it from a commit; the caller hands it the catalogues, which such a site copies beside it. *Accept all* and *Reject optional* are equally prominent, and *Customise* is visible in the banner. Nothing is pre-ticked, and there is no cookie wall. Every page offers *Change privacy settings* and *Withdraw consent*. A choice, a refusal included, holds 12 months, in the consent's own local storage item with the time, the revision and each state, and a random consent ID where the page sends records; a page that sends none makes and keeps no consent ID, which nobody could look up. A site whose description names `consent.domain`, a domain its host is at or below and never a public suffix or shared domain of the Public Suffix List, or a name above one, keeps the choice in the cookie `konzilo-consent` on that domain, `Path=/`, `Secure` and `SameSite=Lax`, readable by script, for the same 12 months, so that every site below the domain reads and writes one choice; the records that wait to be sent stay in the origin's local storage, which keeps the cookie small. Only a release built for the launched origin does so; the pre-launch address, previews, share links and local runs keep a choice of their own, and a release whose host is not at or below the domain keeps its own and warns. The sites must show the same consent revision, or each would ask again about the other's, which one site's registry serving them all gives. A choice kept the other way is not carried over: the visitor is asked once more. The signal is `window.konziloConsent`, with one `konzilo:consent` event at every choice and at every change of the objection. Dates, times, numbers and countries are formatted in one locale per language of the core's words, `en-GB`, `sv-SE`, and the language itself for another catalogue, unless the caller gives its own, by the build and the browser alike, so that one declaration shows one style. Withdrawing removes what the optional services stored that the page reaches, cookies on the shared domain too, shows a notice from the consent texts in the banner's place, and reloads two seconds later; it keeps no choice, so the reloaded page asks again with the banner, as at a first visit, and the next choice has a new consent ID. Every page removes, as it loads, what each optional service the choice does not allow stored that it reaches, so that what another site below the domain stored is gone at the visitor's next page view there. The banner stays hidden on the privacy policy and the cookie policy, where it would cover on a narrow screen the text a visitor wants to read before choosing; the footer controls work there. The components carry no style of their own.
- **Pages Konzilo does not build** show the same consent from the same registry, so that one choice holds on both, through a consent script per language. Every public build writes it at `/_konzilo/consent/<language>.js`, the language's prefix on a site in several, else its language subtag, with a default stylesheet beside it at `/_konzilo/consent/consent.css`, which needs nothing of the page's and which the page may replace with its own on the same classes. Both are served with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=300`, so that a new revision reaches a page cached for days within minutes; a script element needs no CORS. The other page needs one `<script src="…" defer>`, its own footer buttons marked `data-konzilo-consent-action` `change` and `withdraw`, hidden until the script shows them, an element marked `data-konzilo-consent-declaration`, whose own content stays where the script does not run, `<meta name="konzilo-consent" content="legal-page">` on the privacy policy and the cookie policy, and its embeds in the markup of ADR 0024. It may name its own privacy policy and cookie policy in its head, `<link rel="privacy-policy">`, the link type registered for it, and `<link rel="cookie-policy">`, which mirrors it since none is registered, each with an optional `title`, and every link of the consent to a policy then leads to the page's own; else to the site's, as the script was made. The script adds the banner, the notice, the dialog and the placeholders' templates to the end of the body, fills the declaration, gives the signal as soon as it has run and the event at every change, loads nothing else and runs no inline code. When the site does not answer, the page shows no banner and loads nothing optional. Those pages send no records, since the service takes a record only from the site's own pages, so they make and show no consent ID. `consentScript()` gives the script's source, which Konzilo's build writes and a site Konzilo does not build can write with its own build; `docs/site-design.md` holds the page's contract, a Matomo snippet's on such a page among it.
- **The embed panel is the placeholder:** an allowed service loads at once, without a reload. Otherwise the panel asks for consent to that service alone, says where its data goes and, only where its facts list what it stores, that it may store data in the browser, as the cookie declaration does, links to the cookie policy, and offers *Allow and play*.
- **Records** go through the service for every choice, sent in the background and never waited for, to `/_konzilo/consent`, which the service takes in its role for visitors (ADR 0044). A record holds the random consent ID, the time, the revision, the states, how the choice was made, the page's path and language, and no IP address or user agent. The service refuses a record of a revision the site has not published, or whose categories and services are not exactly that revision's, or that did not come from the site's own pages. Each revision is kept with what the visitor saw: each public build writes it, with what its pages showed in each language, beside the release's pages, and the service keeps it from the release in use as soon as the release goes live, before it decides on a record. A record not stored waits beside the choice and is sent once more. Over 3600 records an hour, or over 100 of one client within ten minutes, a record is counted, not stored. Records are kept 13 months, and then forgotten by the service's timed duty in its role for editors, overwritten where they stood, as a submission is. An Admin's page, *Consent records*, shows the choices per day, finds a consent ID's records with what their revision showed, and exports a span of days as CSV, in UTC; it is an Admin's alone, an Editor told only that, as on the *Mail* page.
- **Departures** come only from a closed list in the site description, `consent.departures`, each with a required reason: `tagManagerBeforeConsent`, `necessaryService` and `statisticsWithoutStorageBeforeConsent`. The last has the statistics service count every visit from the first page view without storage: nothing stored in the visitor's browser, and none of the device's properties read, until the visitor allows statistics; then it stores in the same page view, without a reload, and on every later page view while the choice holds, and a withdrawal removes its storage and has it count without storage again. Its `privacySignals`, required, says whether a browser that sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track is not counted at all, `stop`, even after consent, or counted the same way, `ignore`. It applies only to a statistics service with such a mode, Matomo's own tracker (ADR 0050); the build warns of it without one. Counting without storage follows the server-log principle: before consent the statistics count only what a web server's log would hold, what the browser sends with every request anyway, with the page's address, the page it came from and the clock, the only things the script reads; no property of the device, no load time and not its time zone, so that the time of day goes in the site's (ADR 0050). The statistics category then takes its own default description, `statisticsWithoutStorageDescription`, and the cookie declaration says what is counted without storage. Counting without storage stays rejected as the default.
- **The right to object** comes with that departure, since the counting rests on the site's legitimate interest (GDPR Article 21), and a visitor counted without an identifier cannot be found to honour an objection sent by mail. The dialog's statistics category ends in one paragraph that explains it, whose own word is the link, *object*, marked in its text by square brackets, `… you have the right to [object].`, as every text of the objection that holds a link marks it; a text without brackets has the link after it, in the core's words. `consentControlsMarkup()` gives the same for a page, such as the privacy policy, to place, and so does the cookie declaration beside its buttons. An editor places it in a post's body by the core's leaf directive `::consent-objection`, alone on its line with no attributes, the body element `consent-objection` that a type lists among its `elements`, which the build draws there as `consentControlsMarkup()` draws it with `objection` and neither links nor buttons; on a site without the departure it draws nothing, and the intake warns of it in the post. A page's Markdown alternate leaves the right to object out. The link opens a confirmation with a plain appeal, which never says *anonymous*, and two buttons alike. Confirming keeps the objection beside the choice, in the same item, cookie or local storage, on the same domain, for the same 12 months from when it was made: `objection`, its time, with the choice, or alone before any choice. It is not a consent: it makes no consent ID, sends no record of its own, and changes no revision. From then on the tracker sends nothing, in the same page view too, and Matomo sets no cookie for it. Then the explanation's link is plain text, and a second paragraph shows, *You have objected to the counting, so your visits are not counted.*, with its link, *Undo your objection*, which takes the objection back at once, with no confirmation; counting starts again from the next page view. While the choice allows statistics, nothing of the objection shows, neither the explanation, its link nor the confirmation: consent stands, and no legitimate interest needs explaining. It follows the choice kept, and changes as the choice does. A choice that allows a statistics service ends it, and Withdraw consent clears it with every consent: the visitor is then counted without storage as at a first visit, and the banner asks again. A new revision does not end it. The signal's `objected()` says whether the visitor objects. [`docs/counting-visits-before-consent.md`](../counting-visits-before-consent.md) is the site owner's guide to the departure and the objection. Prior consent otherwise, equal prominence, nothing pre-ticked, withdrawal on every page and asking again on a change can never be departed from.
- **The texts** are a core settings group, `consent`, each default a text of the catalogues in the content's language (ADR 0066), as are the core's own words of the consent and the facts of its services.
- **The banner's default is written from the facts** of the services that ask the visitor for consent, by the markup functions, so that a page Konzilo builds and the consent script for one it does not build say the same, in the page's language: what the site needs to work; that with consent it also uses other services, which may store data in the browser only where one of them lists what it stores, use what they learn for their own purposes only where one of them is marketing, and send data to countries outside the EU and EEA only where one of them lists a transfer, each said only where so, as a list; and how to change or withdraw consent. The necessary services' facts say nothing to it. A banner text an Admin has written is shown as it is written instead. The setting `bannerText` has no default, so that `settings.json` and the registry hold it only where an Admin wrote one, and a text a site saved that is a default the core once had is taken as none (`formerDefaults`).

## Why

- One registry keeps the banner, the declaration and the policy in agreement.
- A banner that says every service may store data, profile and transfer it, whatever the site's services do, says more than the site does; written from the facts it says what the placeholder and the declaration say.
- HTML rendered at the build can be styled and tested, and needs nothing the policy has to allow. Functions with no Astro in them render it the same for any site.
- The core's own words come from its catalogues, so that a language is one file to translate, and with the input, so that the folder still imports nothing.
- A reason in the site repository is the record of a deliberate departure.
- A privacy policy explains the counting in its own words, and the right to object belongs beside them, where an editor writes them, not only where a template can place it.
- 13 months covers every consent while it holds.

## Considered and rejected

- **CookieConsent v3.**
- **Departures as a setting in the editing tool.**
- **Records kept 3 years, or 30 days.**
- **A free list of policy sources.**
- **Cookieless analytics before consent as the default.**
- **An objection by mail.** Nothing counted without an identifier can be found again.
- **An objection offered beside consent to statistics.** With consent there is no legitimate interest to object to; withdrawing consent is the way out.
- **Privacy signals as the only objection.** A site may count regardless of them, and a visitor who sends none has no way to object.
- **Matomo's own opt-out, `optUserOut` or `forgetConsentGiven`.** Each writes a cookie of Matomo's own, on its own lifetime and domain, beside the consent's.
- **Google Consent Mode v2, TCF and a scanner, for now.**
