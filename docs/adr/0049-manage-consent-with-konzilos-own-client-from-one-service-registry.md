# ADR 0049: Manage consent with Konzilo's own client, from one service registry

**Date:** 2026-10-29 · **Status:** Accepted. Settled by Thomas on 2026-10-29: the GDPR and ePrivacy to the letter, deliberate departures recorded as such, only the EU's rules, and Real Cookie Banner as the yardstick. The rest is a technical choice; Thomas may veto.

## Decision

- **Categories:** necessary, functional, statistics and marketing.
- **The service registry** is one JSON content file, `consent.json`, which only the intake writes, at every build, and Astro reads as the collection `consent`. It holds the consent's own storage, a local storage item kept 12 months, and with a shared domain a cookie on it too; the embed services the published posts use, from core facts that each name a source in the provider's own documentation and the day it was checked, a provider that uses what it learns for its own advertising or profiling, or whose player stores what another uses so, as Screen9's does Google's ad cookies, making its service marketing, any other functional; the services core features add; and the services an Admin declares in `services.json`, each with its id made from its name once and kept, so that a departure can name it. One the site cannot publish keeps the last registry. Each service has its provider, purpose, storage, retention, hosts per kind of request, and transfers out of the EU and EEA. Each stored item names the domain it is on: the host of the page that stores it, a domain shared with the sites below it, or its provider's, the host its service shows in or loads from where the facts name none. The dialog and the cookie declaration show it.
- **Hosts reach the policy only through a service.** A host an Admin declares is an `https://` origin, `*.` allowed for subdomains, never a bare `*`, a scheme alone, a path, a query or a port, nor one of the site's own hosts. A `*.` never takes in a public suffix or shared domain of the Public Suffix List, or a name above one. The list is pinned in `versions.env` at a commit, and raised with each release.
- **The consent revision** is a hash of the categories, the services and the departures with their reasons, not the wording. A new revision asks every visitor again. The same inputs give the same bytes.
- **Konzilo's own client.** Pure functions render the banner, a native `<dialog>`, the footer controls, the placeholders and the cookie declaration, each an escaped HTML string from the registry, the consent texts, the legal pages and the page's language (`src/consent/markup.js`). Astro's components are thin wrappers around them, and a page Konzilo does not build can call them. One script holds the signal (`src/consent/client.js`), with no library and no inline code. `src/consent/` imports nothing outside itself, so another site can copy it from a commit. *Accept all* and *Reject optional* are equally prominent, and *Customise* is visible in the banner. Nothing is pre-ticked, and there is no cookie wall. Every page offers *Change privacy settings* and *Withdraw consent*. A choice, a refusal included, holds 12 months, in the consent's own local storage item with the time, the revision and each state, and a random consent ID where the page sends records; a page that sends none makes and keeps no consent ID, which nobody could look up. A site whose description names `consent.domain`, a domain its host is at or below and never a public suffix or shared domain of the Public Suffix List, or a name above one, keeps the choice in the cookie `konzilo-consent` on that domain, `Path=/`, `Secure` and `SameSite=Lax`, readable by script, for the same 12 months, so that every site below the domain reads and writes one choice; the records that wait to be sent stay in the origin's local storage, which keeps the cookie small. Only a release built for the launched origin does so; the pre-launch address, previews, share links and local runs keep a choice of their own, and a release whose host is not at or below the domain keeps its own and warns. The sites must show the same consent revision, or each would ask again about the other's, which one site's registry serving them all gives. A choice kept the other way is not carried over: the visitor is asked once more. The signal is `window.konziloConsent`, with one `konzilo:consent` event at every choice. Withdrawing removes what the optional services stored that the page reaches, cookies on the shared domain too, shows a notice from the consent texts in the banner's place, and reloads two seconds later; the banner does not come back, since the choice of the necessary alone holds. Every page removes, as it loads, what each optional service the choice does not allow stored that it reaches, so that what another site below the domain stored is gone at the visitor's next page view there. The banner stays hidden on the privacy policy and the cookie policy, where it would cover on a narrow screen the text a visitor wants to read before choosing; the footer controls work there. The components carry no style of their own.
- **The embed panel is the placeholder:** an allowed service loads at once, without a reload. Otherwise the panel asks for consent to that service alone, says what it may store and where its data goes, links to the cookie policy, and offers *Allow and play*.
- **Records** go through the service for every choice, sent in the background and never waited for. A record holds the random consent ID, the time, the revision, the states, how the choice was made, the page's path and language, and no IP address or user agent. The service refuses a record of a revision the site has not published, or whose categories and services are not exactly that revision's, or that did not come from the site's own pages. Each revision is kept with what the visitor saw. A record not stored waits beside the choice and is sent once more. Over 3600 records an hour, or over 100 of one client within ten minutes, a record is counted, not stored. Records are kept 13 months. An Admin's page shows the choices per day, finds a consent ID's records with what their revision showed, and exports a span of days as CSV, in UTC.
- **Departures** come only from a closed list in the site description, `consent.departures`, each with a required reason: `tagManagerBeforeConsent` and `necessaryService`. Prior consent otherwise, equal prominence, nothing pre-ticked, withdrawal on every page and asking again on a change can never be departed from.
- **The texts** are a core settings group, `consent`, each default in English and Swedish.

## Why

- One registry keeps the banner, the declaration and the policy in agreement.
- HTML rendered at the build can be styled and tested, and needs nothing the policy has to allow. Functions with no Astro in them render it the same for any site.
- A reason in the site repository is the record of a deliberate departure.
- 13 months covers every consent while it holds.

## Considered and rejected

- **CookieConsent v3.**
- **Departures as a setting in the editing tool.**
- **Records kept 3 years, or 30 days.**
- **A free list of policy sources.**
- **Cookieless analytics before consent as the default.**
- **Google Consent Mode v2, TCF and a scanner, for now.**
