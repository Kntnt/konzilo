# ADR 0032: Preview live beside the form, and share a draft through a secret link

**Date:** 2026-11-02, amended 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-12 · **Status:** Accepted. Settled by Thomas: a live full-page preview (2026-11-02); a click in it leads to its block in the body field (2026-11-05), while the focus stays in the preview (2026-11-10); an editor can share a draft with someone who has no login, through a secret link that lasts a few days and can be revoked (2026-10-27); anyone who may see the preview browses it in a tab of its own, at its address, signed in through the site's Forgejo, for as long as they stay signed in there, with the right asked again at intervals (2026-11-11); the preview shows every saved post, whatever its status and moment, and names a saved post's refusal where its page would be (2026-11-11); a tab of its own has a toolbar on every page, which says the page's status, leads someone who may edit to its form on the admin host, starts in the bottom right corner, is dragged anywhere, tucks into a tab on the left or right edge and keeps its place in that browser (2026-11-11). Technical choices, which Thomas may veto (2026-11-12): the tab signs in as an OAuth client of the preview's own, which asks for `read:repository` alone, and whose code the gate exchanges and whose token it uses once; the right is asked again every ten minutes, in a tab by the next page opened and in the pane by its next draft, and a session lasts an hour after its last check for what cannot be led to a sign-in; the gate's own pages are in the site's interface language; a page asked for while the preview is stopped answers `503` with a notice that loads itself again every two seconds; whoever may read the source repository may see the preview in a tab of its own, a Reviewer among them ([ADR 0008](0008-give-customers-three-roles-admin-editor-and-reviewer.md)), while the pane is for whoever may write to it, and only a session of someone who may write sends a draft; a post on its own way keeps an address that a post shown for the preview alone wants too, as it would once the other is published; a saved post's refusal is a page of the gate's in the interface language, answered `200` to a tab at the address its kept page has and at the one it would have, while a frame keeps the last good page; what is saved is read before each page asked for in a tab, from Forgejo at most every ten seconds, and a run with no new commit and no draft changed reads nothing; should the check break on a saved post, the preview goes on with what is published and the drafts being edited, as before; the gate puts the toolbar into each page it shows a tab of its own, so that no build of the site holds it, in a shadow tree of its own, which the site's styles do not reach; a page's status is its post's own, as the form shows it, scheduled while its moment has not come whatever the status says, else published where it is not a draft, and that of the form someone is editing it in where they are; a scheduled post's moment is said in the reader's own form and time zone, the browser's, named, as the *Submissions* page says its moments and the editor a refusal's ([ADR 0045](0045-let-a-developer-make-each-form-and-an-editor-choose-among-them.md), [ADR 0066](0066-translate-the-editors-texts-through-catalogues-in-an-interface-language-of-its-own.md), [ADR 0069](0069-publish-at-once-a-scheduled-post-whose-moment-has-passed-and-say-so.md)), which is what the ticket's "site's time zone" comes to, since a site declares none; a term's page leads to the term's form, a list's later page to its post's or its term's, the 404 page, which the dev server answers `404` or `410` wherever nothing else answers, to the form of the post an Admin chose for it, in the language whose prefix its address is below, where one is chosen, and every other page says it is not edited in one place and leads to the editor's start; the page that names a saved post's refusal has the toolbar too, its one script allowed by its hash; the link opens the entry by its collection and ID, `#/collections/<collection>/entries/<ID>`; the toolbar is kept in the browser's storage for the preview's host by its distance from the two nearer edges of the window, and starts in its corner wherever that storage cannot be read or written; it tucks away when a drag pushes it 32 pixels past an edge, or against the edge with the pointer as far as the window goes, or an arrow key a step past it, and its handle's arrow keys move it 16 pixels, 64 with Shift. The rest is a technical choice; Thomas may veto.

## Decision

**The live preview.**

- A preview template in Sveltia sends the draft, debounced, to the preview's gate. The gate runs the intake with `preview` naming every saved post and the draft, which stands over its saved version, each checked and written whatever its status and date, into the preview's own content folder, and answers with the draft's refusals and its page's address. Astro's dev server has the page drawn again in an iframe, with no empty frame between ([ADR 0067](0067-draw-the-live-previews-page-again-under-a-view-transition-and-keep-its-scroll.md)), laid out at 1,280 pixels and scaled down to a narrower panel, never up: a wider one shows the page at its own size, as wide as the panel ([ADR 0064](0064-let-the-preview-stand-where-an-editor-wants-it-and-show-a-field-its-block-and-back.md)). The scroll position stays. A refused draft leaves the last good page, with the reason above it. Two editors' drafts stand side by side, and one nobody has touched for 15 minutes goes.
- **Every saved post.** The preview shows the site as it will be once everything saved is published: a draft and a scheduled post, whatever their moment, have their page and stand in every post list and recommended reading that would hold them, while the public site's build leaves them out until they are published. A post on its own way, published with its moment come, keeps an address that one shown for the preview alone wants too, even where the preview showed the other there first, since that is what publishing it would meet; the other is refused. Should the check itself break on a saved post, the preview goes on with what is published and the drafts being edited, and the break is logged.
- **A refusal where the page would be.** A saved post the intake refuses leaves the rest of the preview as it is. A page asked for in a tab at its address, the address its last good page in the preview has or the one it would have, which nothing else holds, is a page of the gate's in the interface language that names the post and each refusal, by its field or block, and nothing of the site; it loads nothing and runs nothing but the toolbar, its one script allowed by its hash. A post whose address cannot be told, its slug or its parent refused, is named in its form alone. A frame keeps the last good page, which its pane names the refusal above.
- **What is saved shows at the next page.** A page asked for in a tab brings the preview up to what is saved before it is shown: the checkout asks Forgejo at most every ten seconds, and the intake runs again only for a new commit or a draft changed, so that browsing costs nothing while nothing changes. A dev server's page then reloads itself on the change, as it does for a draft.
- **A toolbar in a tab of its own.** The gate puts a small bar last in each page it shows a tab of its own, in the interface language, in a shadow tree of its own: what the page is built from, and, for someone who may write, a link that opens it on the admin host, in the same tab. A post's page says its status, *published*, *a draft* or *scheduled* with its moment, which the page says in its reader's own form and time zone, named, and leads to the post's form; a term's page, and a list's later pages, lead to the term's or the post's; the 404 page, built from the one post an Admin chose for it ([ADR 0040](0040-answer-unknown-addresses-with-a-chosen-page-and-count-them-without-the-visitor.md)), leads to that post's form, with its status, wherever the dev server answers it; any other page, built from many posts or from settings, or a 404 page with no post chosen, says that it is not edited in one place and leads to the editor's start. Someone who may not write, a Reviewer, sees the status and no link. A frame, which is the pane's, what a page asks for itself and what is not HTML get none, and it does nothing in a frame should one reach it, so the pane never shows it; nor does the public site or a share link's build, which the gate does not serve. The gate asks the dev server for such a page whole, unpacked and anew, so that it can put the toolbar in, and says its new length.
- **The toolbar moves out of the way.** It starts 16 pixels from the bottom right corner. A mouse, a finger or a pen drags it by any part of it once a press has moved a few pixels, wherever the pointer then goes, so that a click or a tap on its link follows it and a drag by the link follows nothing; its handle, a button, moves it by the arrow keys. Pushed against the left or right edge, by a drag 32 pixels past it, or against it with the pointer as far as the window goes, as a mouse is in a window as wide as the screen, or by an arrow key a step past it, it tucks away into a small tab on that edge, at its height, a button that brings it back a little way from the edge, the focus going with it. Its place is kept in the browser's storage for the preview's host, across pages and visits, by its distance from the window's two nearer edges, so that it stays by its corner as the window changes; where that storage cannot be read or written, it starts in its corner on each page and moves there all the same. It is a region named *Live preview* to a screen reader, its handle described by how it moves, and it is hidden in print.
- **One program in the `preview` container,** `bin/konzilo-preview.js`, holds the gate, the runs and the dev server, and shares the web server's network, so that the web server alone reaches it. It reads the source through a checkout outside the site's root, with a credential of its own that can only read, in a volume of its own. Being alone there, it needs nothing of the Node service, and a fault in one does not touch the other.
- **On demand.** Its dev server starts when an editor opens an entry, or someone opens a page in a tab of its own, and stops after 15 minutes idle, and the container runs under its limits. A server setting caps how many run at once, where many sites share a server, which is the hosting layer's. Past the cap, the pane uses a static preview build instead, at 1–3 seconds per pause.
- **Its own origin,** `preview.<site>.konzilo.app`, since the admin host holds a write token in the browser and a page there must never show editor-written content. Only a browser that has shown access to the source through Forgejo gets a session, in a host-only cookie: the pane by the editor's credential, which may write to the source repository, and a tab of its own by Forgejo's sign-in, whose person may read it, as a Reviewer may ([ADR 0008](0008-give-customers-three-roles-admin-editor-and-reviewer.md)). A session holds whether its person may write, and only one that may sends a draft, so a Reviewer changes nothing anyone sees. Nothing here keeps the token it was shown, so the browser holds no secret of Forgejo's here. Every answer carries `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow` and `Cache-Control: private`, and `frame-ancestors` for the admin host alone in place of the framing the site's own headers would send, which would refuse the pane.
- **A tab of its own.** A page asked for in a tab with no session is sent, with `303`, to Forgejo's sign-in, as the preview's own OAuth client, public and with PKCE as the editor's is (ADR 0007), whose one address is the gate's `/_konzilo/preview/sign-in`. Someone already signed in to Forgejo passes through it without typing anything, since the consent page answers itself; anyone else signs in with both factors first. The gate exchanges the code itself, with the verifier only it holds, asks Forgejo with the token what its holder may do with the source repository, and forgets the token. One who may read it gets a session, which holds whether they may write to it as well, and is sent back, with `303`, to the address asked for, a path on this host alone; one who may not gets a plain page in the interface language that says the site's Admins can give access, and nothing of the site. A sign-in is bound to the browser that began it by a cookie of its own for that sign-in, for the sign-in's address alone and ten minutes, so that a sign-in finished elsewhere opens nothing, and tabs that sign in at once keep theirs; each is taken once. One that does not complete says so, with a link to try again. Only a page asked for in a tab is led to a sign-in, `GET` with `Sec-Fetch-Dest: document`, or, from a browser that does not say, one that takes HTML: a frame cannot be led anywhere, and what a page asks for itself has no address bar to come back to.
- **Asked again at intervals.** Whether a session's person may still see the preview is asked again every ten minutes. In a tab the next page opened after that goes through Forgejo's sign-in again, which passes at once while the person is signed in there, so that the session lasts as long as the Forgejo sign-in, and someone removed from the site's teams is refused at the next check, their session ended. In the pane the next draft is refused with `401`, and the pane opens a session anew with the editor's credential. What cannot be led to a sign-in, a frame, what a page asks for itself and the dev server's socket, goes on for an hour after the last check.
- **A visit starts it.** A page asked for in a tab while the dev server is not answering starts it, as opening an entry does, and is answered `503` with a short notice in the interface language that loads itself again every two seconds, until the page is ready; a start that fails is told once, to the page that looks next within a minute, with a link to try again. A frame and a page's own requests wait for it, as they did. The gate's own pages load nothing and run nothing (`default-src 'none'`), and are in the site's interface language, since an editor's own choice is in the admin host's storage, which this host cannot read.
- **The page stays.** A frame is not a browser: whatever would load something else in it would take the editor away from what they are working on, so nothing does. In a tab of its own the site behaves as it does for a visitor: links, forms and the browser's history lead on as on the public site, and a form still says that a preview cannot send. Inside the pane alone, a guard in each of the live preview's pages stops a link, a form, an assignment to `location`, a refresh in a tag, a new window and a step in the browser's history, which could otherwise take the editor around the frame with it, in WebKit, and tells the pane where it was going. A reload stays, since it is how the dev server shows a change, and so do the page replacing itself with its own address, the same path and query, by which that change is drawn without an empty frame (ADR 0067), and a step within the page. No script can stop a redirect, so the gate answers a frame's request for one with `204`, which leaves the frame as it is, while a tab of its own and a page's own requests follow theirs.
- **The pane says what would have happened.** For a few seconds, over the page, so that nothing on it moves, it says where a link goes on the public site, and, from the gate, which asks the dev server, the same rules as the public site's, what a visitor would meet there: a redirect's last target, no page, or a circle. A draft's own address is looked at before the frame goes there, and one that redirects is never shown: the last good page stays, with where it leads above it.
- **Nothing goes round.** The frame never follows a redirect, so no page runs again to try anything again. A draft's address is looked at once, and asking where an address leads stops at a circle, or after ten steps. A page's script that keeps trying is told of once for each address, and a few times in all, each attempt being stopped either way. A click is told of each time, since a person made it. A page that reloads itself, by a script or a refresh in a tag, is the one thing left alone: it loads nothing else, and looks like the dev server's own reload.
- **A click on the page shows its block in the body field.** In the live preview alone, each block the body's Markdown makes carries `data-konzilo-block` with its number, each footnote counting as one, and a component or a list of posts gets a wrapper of its own with the same, which a site's styles never see (`display: contents`). A script in each page outlines the block under the pointer, and a click that selects nothing posts the block's number and its text to the pane around it, which takes it only from its own frame on the preview's origin. The field scrolls to the block of that number if it begins with the same words, or else to the nearest block that does, since the draft may have changed after the page was made, and frames it, while the focus stays in the preview ([ADR 0064](0064-let-the-preview-stand-where-an-editor-wants-it-and-show-a-field-its-block-and-back.md)). A click on a link, a button or a form's field is left to them.
- **Its gate stands before its certificate exists.**
- **A preview has none of these:** no sitemap, robots, feeds, `llms.txt` or not-found counts, search says it is not there, a form says it is a preview and cannot send, and its policy reports nothing. The gate passes only pages and their assets. Its file access stays inside the site's root, so no secret is in reach; a test proves this. Editors can read the site's code through it, which is accepted.
- **An unsaved image** has a `blob:` address in the draft, so the template sends the file too, and the preview's intake makes its master. The SVG check runs there as well.
- **The preview has a copy of the site's code of its own,** without a local copy's secrets, so that a dev server and a build never share an `.astro/`.
- **A deploy stops the preview** and removes each share's release, so that the next asks anew from the new code.

**Share links.**

- **A link is `https://<preview host>/share/#<secret>`,** never on the admin host, whose browser storage holds the write token and which holds no cookies (ADR 0001, ADR 0004). It is open three days or until revoked, never extended. The secret is 32 random bytes in base64url, in the link's fragment, which a browser never sends to a server. The store holds only its SHA-256, so a copy or a backup opens no draft, and the secret is shown once.
- **Following the link.** `/share/` answers a page that loads nothing (`default-src 'none'`, its one script allowed by its hash), sends no referrer and is never kept. Its script takes the secret out of the address bar and posts it. An open link sets a cookie for the share's own address alone (a `__Secure-` name, since `__Host-` needs the path `/`; `Path=/share/<ID>/`, `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Lax`), expiring with the link, and leads on with 303. The service answers below `/share/` from the share's static build, so a link never reaches the dev server on that host. Any other secret gets 403.
- **What it shows:** a static preview build of the published posts and that one draft, as its latest saved version, nothing else of any draft, and never the dev server. It is the exact build, under the enforced policy.
- **Checked on every request.** A link refused, expired or revoked gets 403 at the next request, a page with no word of the draft. The log never holds the cookie or the post.
- **A field in the post's form makes, lists and revokes them.** Whoever may edit a post may share it while it is not yet published. Purging a post deletes its links. A customer with market-sensitive information turns them off (ADR 0004).

## Why

- Browsing the preview is for seeing what is coming, which a preview of the published site alone does not show. A draft that wants what a published post holds is the draft's problem once published, so it is in the preview too.
- A dev server is the only way to feel live. Starting it on demand answers its memory cost, an origin of its own and the session answer its reach, and static share links keep it from people without a login.
- A fragment is the only part of an address no server, proxy, log or `Referer` sees.
- A tab must follow the Forgejo sign-in, and only the browser can say whether its person is still signed in there: a sign-in that passes at once while they are is the check, and costs a moment every ten minutes.
- Checked on every request: revoking takes effect at the next page.

## Considered and rejected

- **A static rebuild as the live preview.** Too slow to feel live.
- **The preview on `konzilo.net`.** Safari blocks a cookie in a frame from another site, so the panel would have to become a separate window.
- **The dev server for share links.** It would open the site's source to people without a login.
- **A secret in the path or the query.** In every log, in referrers, and in what a link preview fetches.
- **Signed links that need no store.** One cannot be revoked without storing it after all.
- **The secret stored as it is.** A copy would open drafts.
- **A lifetime the editor chooses.** One more control; a new link gives three more days.
- **Rendering on request.** Templates would read content through another API.
- **The editor's OAuth client for the tab.** Forgejo refuses a sign-in with other scopes than its grant holds, so the tab would have to ask for Sveltia's scopes, which change with Sveltia, and the gate would be given a token that writes.
- **Keeping the token, or its refresh token, to ask again without the browser.** The host would hold a secret, and a refresh token lasts a month, whatever becomes of the Forgejo sign-in.
- **Asking with a machine's credential who is in the site's teams.** It would not follow the Forgejo sign-in, and would put a credential that reads the whole organisation in the preview's container.
- **Asking again at every page.** A round through Forgejo for every page asked for, for a change that comes seldom.
- **The published posts and the drafts being edited alone.** What is coming would not be seen until it was out.
- **A saved post's last good page in a tab, with no word of its refusal.** It would show an earlier draft as what is saved, and say nothing of why the saved one cannot be published.
- **The toolbar in the site's build, as a script of the integration's like the guard's.** Every page the dev server makes would carry it, the pane's too, a share link's build would have to be kept from it, and the page would have to ask the gate who is looking and what it is built from, which the gate knows as it answers.
- **A time zone of the site's own for a scheduled moment.** The site declares none, and the toolbar runs in the reader's browser, which knows the reader's own.
- **A scheduled moment in UTC, as the form holds it.** It reads wrong to a reader elsewhere, as a moment as the intake holds it does in a refusal (ADR 0066).
- **A status apart for a post someone is editing.** The page shows the form's version, and whether it differs from what is saved is not known without comparing the two; the status is the form's, as the page is.
- **Reading the source on a timer while the preview runs.** It would read Forgejo every few seconds with nobody browsing, where a page asked for is the moment someone wants what is saved.

## What practice has shown

- First page after 13.3 s cold, 9.8 s warm, in the read-only hardened container; 590–790 MB, under a 1.5 GiB cap. A written file shows on the page after 1.5–1.8 s.
- An outside address asked for `preview.b…` within about 11 seconds of the proxy's start, on a name told to nobody, probably from the certificate logs.
- A dev server and a build in one root write `.astro/content-assets.mjs` and `content-modules.mjs`, and each loads the other's: 500 on pages with an image, and `LocalImageUsedWrongly` in a build.
- A dev server's pages differ a little from a release's, in bundling and in the policy they send.
- In a frame sandboxed against moving what is around it, `history.back()` still took a whole page back in WebKit 27.2, though not in Chromium 156 or Firefox 157. Every other way out came to the Navigation API's `navigate` event in all three, and could be stopped there, though `userInitiated` does not tell a click from a script alike in each. A link to a new tab or to the frames around comes to no such event: the sandbox refuses it, and says nothing.
- A tab signed in through Forgejo 17 as the preview's own client, with `read:repository` alone, and came back to the address asked for with its session, in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit on `http://localhost`, its cookies `SameSite=Lax`. Forgejo's answer to `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}` with that token gave the person's own `permissions.push`. For someone signed in to Forgejo, the round through it, the consent page answering itself, took well under a second on a local copy.
- A Reviewer's account, whose team reads the source repository and does not write to it, signed in to a tab of its own in Chromium the same way, and saw what an Editor sees there.
- A draft and a scheduled post saved to Forgejo were left out of the public site's release and its feed, and showed in a tab of the preview on their own pages, in the start page's list and in an article's recommended reading, with a third post, whose title the intake refuses, named at its address: about eight seconds from the saves, the public site's build included, on a local copy.
- The toolbar, in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit at 1,000 by 800 pixels, started 16 pixels from the bottom right corner, was dragged by a mouse, by its status and by its link, which it then did not follow, tucked into each edge and brought back by a click, kept its place across pages and in a new tab, and started in its corner on each page where storage threw; it was reached by Tab, Option and Tab in WebKit as in Safari, moved and tucked by the arrow keys, the focus going to its tab and back. On a phone's 390 pixels it fitted, a finger dragged it without scrolling the page, and a tap brought it back and followed its link: a real finger in Chromium, and in Firefox and WebKit, which take no moving finger from Playwright, the finger's pointer events sent to the bar. A drag whose pointer stops at the window's edge, as a mouse does in a window as wide as the screen, pushed the bar too little past it to tuck it, until a pointer at the window's edge was made to tuck it too.
- In a site in Compose, the toolbar said a saved draft was a draft, a scheduled post when it goes live, in UTC, and a published article that it was, named a refused post's form on the page of its refusal, and led from the start page to the editor's; a Reviewer saw the status and no link, and an editor's tab, signed in to Forgejo, led by the link to the article's form in Sveltia, in the same tab, while the pane beside the form had no toolbar.
