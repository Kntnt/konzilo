# ADR 0062: Give an article its parts as the core's fields, and fall back by one set of rules

**Date:** 2026-11-10, amended 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11 · **Status:** Accepted. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-10: the parts are named as Kntnt names an article's parts; the standfirst exists only in an article, and the lede is body text with no field; a category is one or none; whether breadcrumbs end with the page itself is a choice per type, off by default; the article's form follows the article. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-10 too: three built-in types, canvas, page and article, the article's parts fixed by Kntnt's model of an article, each optional to fill in but the main headline, and three built-in taxonomies for it, authors, category and topics, whose shape a site cannot redeclare; an article's authors in the order the editor gives them, while its topics and its category stay sorted, chosen in a field of the core's own that suggests authors as a name is typed, shows each as a chip to drag into order or remove, and adds a new author without leaving the form. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-11: a canvas is *Arbetsyta* in Swedish, as design tools name a canvas. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-11 too: the byline stays a list of the authors' names, without "By"; the meta line, the line between the standfirst and the body that shows the byline, the date, the category and the like, is written by a format with variables by name, set by an Admin in the site settings once for a type and per language, never per post; its default is on the lines of "Published {date} in {category} by {authors}", in correct British English, with a good Swedish translation whose words need not come in the English order; the authors stay a comma-separated list; and every post is published at a moment, which is its date, as in WordPress, and which an editor may change, so a page and a canvas have the date and the last change, the name in breadcrumbs and the teaser, and none of the article's main image, authors, category, topics, headline or standfirst. The rest is a technical choice; Thomas may veto. Among them, the meta line's (2026-11-11): its format in a settings group of its own beside Search and sharing; a variable without a value dropping out with the words before it, back to the variable or the punctuation mark before them, the punctuation it leaves tidied; the last change only where it falls on a later day than the date; the category by its name, linked to its page where it has one; and a format for every type with an address but the canvas, the article's alone with a default, so that a page takes a meta line only where an Admin gives it one, which may name its date and last change, and a canvas, which is its body alone and which the site template draws without one, is offered none. Among them too, a page's and a canvas's (2026-11-11): its teaser, with no text of its own, falls back to none, so that a list shows its title alone and its head's description is its type's template's, by default the start of its body, as an article's without a standfirst is; the teaser's image falls back to the main image only in a type that has one; and every built-in type's head, search and sharing fields and summary follow its parts, since each has a teaser. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-11 too: the structured data type chosen under *Structured data* decides, wherever it can, what a post is, its Open Graph type, its article node and its authors, and each type says which structured data types it offers, an article a few, Article, BlogPosting and NewsArticle, and a page and a canvas practically all. Among the technical choices on that (2026-11-11): a page and a canvas offer all seven the core knows, the kinds of web page first, WebPage their default, since a page may be an about page, a press release or a one-off post alike; a type of the site's own declares its list in its site description, `structuredDataTypes`, its default first, and offers what a page offers where it declares none, while a built-in type's list is the core's; and a post's or a setting's choice that its type does not offer is left out with a warning, so that its type's own applies, rather than refused; and a page, a canvas or a type of the site's own marked as a kind of article, which has no authors, names the publisher as its article's author, as an article without authors does ([ADR 0046](0046-generate-search-and-sharing-metadata-from-templates-an-admin-edits.md)), rather than naming none.

## Decision

- **The parts are fields of the core's own,** beside `date`, `image` and `excerpt`: `breadcrumb`, the name in breadcrumbs; `eyebrow`, `headline` and `subheadline`, the headline in its three parts; `standfirst`; and `teaser`, `{ image: { src, alt }, headline, text }`, each part optional. Each text is plain, refused with HTML in it, and only `headline` and `standfirst` may be required. The built-in article has them all, a page and a canvas the name in breadcrumbs and the teaser; a type of the site's own declares the parts it has, as it declares any field. The title stays: where a type has a main headline, the title is the post's working name, which lists, menus and breadcrumbs show.
- **The name in breadcrumbs is a field of its own,** apart from the search and sharing fields, so that a page and a canvas have it beside them, and a type of the site's own without them.
- **Search, sharing and structured data are three groups of the source's own,** `search`, `sharing` with its image as `{ src, alt }`, and `structured`, each a key of the source file beside the post's fields, since the editor cannot place one object's fields in three places in a form. The intake writes the content file's one `seo` map as before, and refuses a source that still holds `seo`, so that nothing it says is lost without a word.
- **One module says what each part falls back to,** `article-parts.js`, which the editor and the build both import: the teaser's headline is its own, else the eyebrow, a colon and the main headline, else the title; its text is its own, else the standfirst; its image its own, else the main image. A teaser falls back to what its type has: a page's and a canvas's headline to the title, and its text and image to none. A title tag is cut at a word to fit, with the site's separator, between single spaces, and the site's name, within 580 pixels of Arial 20 px bold, and a meta description within 920 pixels of Arial 14 px, as search results show them, by a table of Arial's advance widths read from the font files; a sharing title and description within 55 and 100 characters.
- **A post whose type has a teaser says in its head what its teaser shows,** before the type's templates: its title tag, meta description and sharing texts. Every built-in type has a teaser. Where the teaser has no text, the description and the sharing description are the type's templates', by default `{{ post.auto_description }}`, the start of its body. Its structured data's headline is its main headline, else its title. A type without a teaser is as before.
- **A summary is the excerpt, else the teaser's text, else the standfirst,** and search shows a post by its main headline with that summary.
- **A taxonomy field may take `most`,** the most terms a post takes, which the intake and Astro's schema hold it to. With `most: 1` the editor offers one choice or none, and the source may hold the term's ID alone.
- **Three types are built in,** `canvas`, a blank page, *Arbetsyta* in Swedish, `page`, a canvas with the site's header and footer, and `article`, a page with the article parts fixed, each in `src/format/built-in-types.js`, the one place that says what they are and which fields hold an article's authors, its category and its topics. A site uses one by naming it among its types, and gives it what it gives a type of its own but its fields: its collection, address, feed, elements, body editor and search setting, whether it is hierarchical, which only a page and a canvas may be, and a label and plural of its own, where it wants them, in place of the core's from the catalogues. Every post of a built-in type is published at a moment, its date, which the editor gives or the intake takes as the moment it first took the post, and has its last change, as an article always had. A page and a canvas have the date and the last change, the name in breadcrumbs, the teaser and the search and sharing fields, in the article's order; an article the date and the last change, the main image, the name in breadcrumbs, the headline in its three parts, the standfirst, `authors`, `category`, at most one, `topics`, the teaser and the search and sharing fields, the main headline alone required. A site may add fields of its own, which come before the search and sharing fields; one named as a field of the core's is refused, whether declared again, left out with `false` or `null`, or one the type does not take, such as `excerpt` on an article. The read form gives the type its fields in full and names it in `builtIn`, null on a type of the site's own, so that everything that reads a type's fields reads a built-in one as it is.
- **Naming the article brings its taxonomies,** `authors`, `category`, hierarchical, and `topics`, in the collections `authors`, `categories` and `topics`, each held by the article's field of its name. A site may give each an address, a feed and a label of its own, and nothing of its shape. An author is a term of `authors`: its name, its slug and its biography, the term's description. On a site without an article, a taxonomy of one of these names is the site's own.
- **An article's authors keep the order the editor gave them,** each once, in its content file, while every other terms field is sorted; its structured data names them in that order ([ADR 0046](0046-generate-search-and-sharing-metadata-from-templates-an-admin-edits.md)), and a template is given them so for its byline, by `authorsOf(entry)` from `@kntnt/konzilo/astro/helpers`, each with its name, its page's address where the authors taxonomy has one, and its term. The site template's article shows the byline in its meta line.
- **A post's meta line is written by its type's format,** the line between its standfirst and its body that shows its byline, its date and its category. An Admin sets the format per type with an address and per language, in the settings group `metaLine`, beside Search and sharing, as `metaLine.types.<type>.format`, never per post, so every post of a type shows the same; the canvas, which is its body alone, has no format, and the settings neither offer nor keep one for it. A format is text with variables in braces, by name, since the order of the words differs between languages: `{authors}`, the authors in the editor's order with a comma between, each linked to its page where the authors taxonomy declares an address, and `{category}`, the category's name, linked to its page where the category taxonomy declares one, each an article's; and `{date}`, the day the post was published, and `{modified}`, the day it last changed where that is a later day, each a type's whose posts have it, as a long date in the page's language. The intake refuses a variable the type has not got, naming it. The article's default is a text of the catalogues, `Published on {date} in {category} by {authors}` in British English and `Av {authors}, publicerad den {date} under {category}` in Swedish; every other type, the page among them, has none, and shows no meta line until an Admin gives it a format. A variable without a value drops out with the words before it, back to the variable before it or the last punctuation mark between them; then punctuation left at the line's start goes, and so does a separator left at its end, the separator of two marks that meet, or else the first, and a pair of brackets left empty, and a line whose first words dropped begins with a capital letter. A template asks `metaLine(entry, Astro.url)` from `@kntnt/konzilo/astro/helpers` for the line's pieces, each `{ text, variable, url, datetime }`. The site template's article shows it after its standfirst, in place of a byline, a date and a category of its own, and its page after its heading; its canvas, which is its body alone, shows none.
- **The authors field is the core's own,** `konzilo-authors`, in place of Sveltia's relation, and the source keeps its value, the authors' IDs, as a list in the order chosen. A box suggests the authors not chosen yet whose names hold what is typed, case and marks aside, and opens to list them all, in the order of their names in the interface language; the arrow keys go through the suggestions and Enter takes one. Each chosen author is a chip, dragged into its place, or moved with the arrow keys, Home and End on it, and removed with its own control or Delete. *Add a new author* opens a dialog with a name and a biography, commits the author's file at once, and chooses the author, all without leaving the form. The field reads the authors' files from Forgejo with the editor's own credential, as the image library is read ([ADR 0055](0055-choose-images-in-a-body-from-a-library-and-copy-their-texts-into-the-post.md)), from the main language's folder on a site in several, where every author has a file. A category and topics stay Sveltia's relation, which adds a term in the form too.
- **Whether a post is an article is whether the structured data type it has is a kind of article,** Article, BlogPosting or NewsArticle (`isKindOfArticle()`): its `og:type` and its article node in the structured data, with its authors where its type has them ([ADR 0046](0046-generate-search-and-sharing-metadata-from-templates-an-admin-edits.md)). The type it has is its own choice, else its type's from the settings, else its type's default, each only among those its type offers (`structuredDataTypeOf()`). Each type offers a list, its default first: the built-in article its kinds of article, Article first; a page and a canvas all seven, the kinds of web page first, WebPage first, then the kinds of article; and a type of the site's own the list it declares, `structuredDataTypes`, else a page's. The settings' choice for a type, and the editor's for a post, are among them, and a choice its type does not offer, in the source or in the settings, is left out with a warning, so that its type's own applies. A page or a canvas marked as an article has its date and the publisher as its author, since neither has authors. Whether a post's type is the built-in article (`isArticle()`) still says which fields it has: its authors, its category and its topics, in its meta line and its related posts, the meta line's default, and its place after the pages in `llms.txt`. A post's head, its search and sharing fields in the editor and its summary follow its parts where its type has a teaser (`followsParts()`): every built-in type, and a type of the site's own that declares one. What the core tied to a type named `page`, the default of a part per type, the posts a setting chooses among and the page for addresses not found, follows the built-in page.
- **A template is told a post's built-in type,** by `builtInType(entry)` from `@kntnt/konzilo/astro/helpers`, null for a type of the site's own; the site template's layout draws a canvas without its header and footer.
- **Whether a page's breadcrumbs end with the page itself is its type's setting,** `current` in the settings group `breadcrumbs`, off by default. `breadcrumbs()` keeps the page's own level, marked `hidden`, so that the structured data holds every level and a design whose narrow form counts on the level can keep it; `Breadcrumbs` leaves it out, and shows nothing where only the start page would be left.

## Why

- An article's parts each have a name in Kntnt's terms, and a site that names them itself names them differently every time. Fields of the core's own let the core say what each falls back to, and let a design style what it is given rather than place it.
- One module for the editor's placeholders and the site's head means a placeholder never promises what the site does not do.
- A search result and a shared link show the teaser, so a type with a teaser has said what they should show.
- The build has no browser to measure a title in, so the widths are a table, the same in the editor and the build.
- A page right under the start page has a trail of the start page alone, which says nothing.
- Every post is published at a moment, as in WordPress, and a page's matters as an article's does: the sitemap's last change, a feed, a list ordered by date. A page and a canvas take the parts that name and show any post, the name in breadcrumbs and the teaser, and none of those that make an article an article.
- A page's teaser with nothing to fall back to but its title says nothing it was not told. Its type's template still gives its head a description, the start of its body by default, as it gives one to an article without a standfirst, and a list that shows a summary shows none rather than a cut paragraph the editor never chose.
- An article with the same parts on every site means an editor learns its form once, a design styles the same parts everywhere, and the core can say who wrote an article and what it is about without guessing from a site's fields.
- Naming a built-in type by its name, rather than by a key on a type of any name, means one name for one thing, and no site with two articles.
- A post is what it says it is to search engines: an editor who marks a page as an article, a press release as a NewsArticle, means it to be read as one, and its type's fields say only which of an article's parts it holds. An article offers only the kinds of article, so it never stops being one.
- A list per type keeps an editor from choosing what the type cannot be. A type of the site's own knows what its posts are, so its site description says it; a built-in type's list is the core's, as its fields are.
- A page or a canvas marked as an article is an article to search engines, which ask an article for an author. Its type has no authors, so the publisher stands behind it, as it stands behind an article whose editor named none, rather than an article node that names no one.
- A choice a type does not offer comes from an earlier list or a hand-edited file, not from the form, and refusing it would hold back a post's other changes until an editor finds it, so it is left out, said in a warning, and the type's own applies, as the editor's field shows.
- The first author of an article is its main author, so the order of its authors is the editor's to give, and every byline and the structured data show it. The order of topics and of a category says nothing, so they stay sorted, and a content file changes only when they do.
- A meta line's words and their order differ between languages and between sites, so it is a format an Admin writes, with variables by name, which may stand in any order, rather than printf's `%s`. It is set once for a type, since a reader knows the line by its being the same on every post.
- The meta line is what the page itself shows, and the group Search and sharing what search engines and social networks show of it, so the format is a group of its own beside it rather than one of its templates.
- In most formats the words before a variable lead into it, "in {category}", "by {authors}", so they drop with it; a format is written as the sentence it is with every value there, so the punctuation a dropped part leaves is tidied rather than left to the Admin. Thomas's Swedish default opens with its authors, so a line whose first words dropped takes the capital they had.
- A last change on the day the post was published tells a reader nothing the date does not.
- A page or a canvas is often a page such as *About us* or a landing page, which a date or a byline would date or sign wrongly, so neither has a meta line by default; the setting is there for a page, so that an Admin who wants "Updated {modified}" on pages writes it, and every type with a date and a format has its `{date}` and `{modified}`, the page among them. A canvas is its body alone, with no heading for a line to stand under, so it is offered no format.
- An order an editor gives needs a field that shows it and changes it, by the mouse and by the keyboard alike. A field of Sveltia's cannot list another collection's entries or make one, so the core's own asks Forgejo, and an author committed at once can be named from the first moment, with nothing more to save.

## Considered and rejected

- **The parts as a site's own fields.** Each site would name them, and the core could not fall back between them.
- **The groups inside one `seo` object in the source.** The editor would have to show them together.
- **Dropping the page's own level when it is not shown.** The structured data and a design's narrow form need it.
- **A site that declares the core's fields of a built-in type,** as it declares its own. Every site would repeat the same list, and could leave a part out.
- **A key that makes a type of any name the article,** such as `"builtIn": "article"`. Two names for one thing, and a site could declare two articles.
- **Whether a post is an article by its type alone,** whatever its structured data type. A page marked as an Article would say one thing in its structured data and another in its sharing tags.
- **An article node with no author for a page or a canvas marked as an article,** since neither has authors. It would be an article that names no one behind it, and credited otherwise than an article of the same site without authors.
- **A choice its type does not offer refused,** as a name the core does not know is. It would hold back the post's other changes after a site narrows a type's list.
- **A type's list on its search and sharing fields,** `"seo": { … }`. A type without them still has a structured data type, from the settings.
- **A type's default as a key of its own beside its list.** The first of the list says it, as a select's first choice does.
- **The built-in taxonomies' names kept from every site.** A site without an article, a product catalogue, may well want a category of its own.
- **Sveltia's relation for the authors, as for topics.** With five authors or fewer it is a box to tick for each, which keeps the order they were ticked in and has no way to change it; with more, a list whose choices can be reordered. How an editor set the order would hang on how many authors a site has.
- **A new author kept with the post until it is saved, as Sveltia's relation keeps one.** No public extension point lets a field put another entry into Sveltia's save; only a patch of its minified build could.
- **Square brackets around the words that belong to a variable,** such as `Published on {date}[ in {category}]`. Exact, but every Admin would learn a syntax for what nearly every format needs, and Thomas's own defaults read rightly without it. A language whose words follow what they name, as a postposition does, has them written before it instead.
- **The format among Search and sharing's templates per type,** `search.types`. That group says what search engines and social networks show, and its templates' `{{ }}` would read as the same kind of text.
- **A default meta line for a page and a canvas.** A date under the heading of *About us* says the page is news.
- **A meta line's format offered for the canvas.** A canvas is its body alone, a landing page built of components, which the site template draws without a heading or a meta line; a format there would change nothing a visitor sees.
- **"By" before the byline in the core's markup.** The byline is the names; the words around them are the format's, in the page's language.
- **Buttons on each chip to move it, as a gallery's rows have.** Three controls on a chip of a name crowd a field that may hold several; the arrow keys on the chip itself move it, and its description says so to whoever hears the page rather than sees it.

## Consequences

- A content file's `seo` map holds no `breadcrumb`, and a source file holds the three groups, never `seo`, which the intake refuses rather than lose what it says.
- An article whose type an Admin had given a kind of web page, or whose own choice was one, is an Article again, with a warning naming the choice; a type of the site's own an Admin had given a kind of article is an article in its `og:type` too, with its date.
- A site's own type with a teaser falls back as an article does, and one without leaves its head to its templates. A page and a canvas fall back as an article does too, to their title, so their title tag is the title, the site's separator and the site's name, and the site settings offer none of the three a title tag or sharing title template.
- A page's and a canvas's content file holds its date and its last change, which the reader requires as an article's: a page's file written by an earlier core, without them, is one the reader no longer takes, and the next build, which writes every file again, writes it with them, its date the moment the intake first took the post, as the ledger keeps it. The sitemap gives a page's last change, a page or a canvas may have a feed, a post list of them may be ordered by date, search shows a page's date, its structured data its date and last change, and `llms.txt` lists pages newest first, as it lists every type with a date.
- An article's authors are the terms of its `authors`, which its structured data and its byline name, in the editor's order; its category and topics are what related posts can be chosen by.
- An author added from a post's form stays, as a term, if the post is never saved, and is removed under the authors like any other. Sveltia's own list of authors shows it the next time Sveltia looks at the repository, within a minute.
- The authors field's own texts are under `authors.` in the catalogues and say "author", whatever label a site gives the taxonomy, which the field's own label shows.
- With no Forgejo to ask, as in Sveltia's test repository, the field shows the IDs it holds and offers no new author.
- The site template's site description and demo content are on the built-in types, their subjects among the topics.
- The meta line shows an article's category by its name, without its parents; the site template's article shows its topics as a list, and its category in its meta line alone. A design that wants the byline as a list still has `authorsOf(entry)`.

## What practice has shown

- **Sveltia leaves the entry at an Escape,** which it hears on the window before anything on the page does, whatever has the focus, unless a dialog covers its back button. The authors field's own listener there is set before Sveltia starts, so it hears first, and keeps an Escape while the box has a list to close or a name to clear.
- **Safari's Tab goes from field to field alone,** and Option-Tab to every control, so a chip's own control and *Add a new author* are reached with Option-Tab there.
