# ADR 0037: Give hierarchical types addresses that follow their parents, and derive breadcrumbs

**Date:** 2026-10-29, amended 2026-11-13 and 2026-11-25 · **Status:** Accepted. Settled by Thomas on 2026-10-29: addresses follow parents; breadcrumbs always in the structured data, on screen by choice. Settled by Thomas on 2026-11-25: breadcrumbs follow a post's address and nothing else, a type naming no page above its posts, so that a site that wants its articles under the page that lists them gives the type an address below that page; and each level is named by its post's name in breadcrumbs, else by the label of the menu entry that leads to it in the page's language, of the menus in the order the site description declares them the first that has one, and in it the first, else by its title. The rest is a technical choice; Thomas may veto, among it what the start page an Admin chose is to its children and to breadcrumbs (2026-11-13), and, from 2026-11-25: a menu entry leads to a post where it names the post by its ID, which the menu's content file keeps as `post` beside the post's current address, so that the site and the editor's field read the same entries, and an entry that only links to the post's address, written by hand, leads to no post and names none; the start page's level keeps its own name, the chosen page's name in breadcrumbs, else the site's word for the start page, which no menu's label replaces; and a term's page's own level keeps the term's name.

## Decision

- **Hierarchical types.** A type is one when it declares `"hierarchical": true`. It takes `{path}` in its address, `/{path}/` or `/pages/{path}/`; every other type takes `{slug}`. The checker refuses each the other way round. Before either, an address may hold the post's date, and a type that is not hierarchical the term of a field that takes one, never a hierarchical type, whose posts stand under their parents ([ADR 0080](0080-let-a-types-address-hold-its-posts-date-and-the-term-of-a-field-that-takes-one.md)).
- **A post's path** is its ancestors' slugs, from the top, and its own: `about-us/team/anna`. A parent is a relation by ID, an ordinary field. A draft ancestor gives its slug. An ancestor refused, missing or of another type refuses the post, naming it.
- **Published files** sit at their paths inside the collection's folder, `pages/about-us/team.md` beside `pages/about-us.md`. Astro's entry ID is the path.
- **Moves** are written at the next build: each post's file at its current path, the old files and the folders they leave empty removed. A move touches one source file, and the guard counts it as no deletion. A post that goes live at a new path leaves the old as an automatic `301`.
- **The start page an Admin chose** ([ADR 0033](0033-keep-menus-settings-and-the-sites-identity-in-files-an-admin-edits.md)) is at `/`, and a page with children may be chosen: its children keep their paths, which hold its slug, `/pages/welcome/team/` below Welcome, and their files stay where they were, so that choosing moves no other page. Its own address before leads to `/`, and is no level of their breadcrumbs.
- **Breadcrumb levels:** the start page, named in the page's language, or by the name in breadcrumbs of the page an Admin chose for it where that page has one; each shorter address the page's address starts with at which a published post answers, of any type, a post whose address holds its date or its term answering only where its own values give it that address; then the page itself. Each post, the page itself among them, is named by its name in breadcrumbs, the field `breadcrumb`, else by the label of the menu entry that leads to it, else by its title (`breadcrumbOf()` in `article-parts.js`); a term's page by the term's name. The menu entry is the first that names the post by its ID, not a link to its address, in the menus of the page's language, the menus in the order the site description declares them and each in reading order, an entry before those under it. An address at which no post answers is left out, so a type's prefix has no level of its own.
- **In the structured data,** every page but the start page carries a `BreadcrumbList`.
- **On screen,** `Breadcrumbs.astro` writes `<nav aria-label="Breadcrumbs">` holding an `<ol>`, a link for each level but the last, the last `aria-current="page"`, with no styling of its own. A site's design places it. Whether the last level is the page itself is the type's setting, on by default for a hierarchical type and off for any other, and on for a post whose name in breadcrumbs is filled in whatever its type, and `breadcrumbs()` keeps the level, marked hidden ([ADR 0062](0062-give-an-article-its-parts-as-the-cores-fields-and-fall-back-by-one-set-of-rules.md)).
- **One function** gives the levels to the head and the component alike, and reads each collection once in a build, since Astro's `getEntry()` warns of every entry it does not find.

## Why

- Two pages may share a slug under different parents, and the content repository shows the tree.
- A level is a real page, so no breadcrumb leads nowhere.
- A visitor knows a post by the word the menu leading to it uses, *Articles* rather than the page's longer title, so the breadcrumbs say the same, while an editor who wants another word for them writes it in the post. The name in breadcrumbs stays empty unless written, so a later change of the menu or the title changes the breadcrumbs with it.
- Where a post sits is its address, which an editor and a visitor both see; a level that only a type declared would put a post under a page its address does not hold.

## Considered and rejected

- **Flat files with a `parent` field.** They collide on slugs that siblings in different branches share.
- **Levels cut from the address string.** They name segments that have no page.
- **A type naming a page above its posts,** such as the page that lists its articles. A post would sit in its breadcrumbs where its address does not put it; a type whose address is below that page, `/resources/library/{slug}/`, puts it there in both.
- **The menu's label copied into the name in breadcrumbs.** A later change of the menu would leave the copy stale; the field shows the label in its place while it is empty, as the article parts show their fallbacks.
- **A menu's label for the start page's level.** The start page is named by the site's word for it, which a site may give in its own texts, and an entry leading to it is often a logo or a word chosen for the menu alone.
